# Spaces

A space groups users and workspaces: it is a key, a display name, a list of members and a listing of the workspaces that belong to it. Every user has one **personal space**, and any signed-in user may create **team spaces**. Every workspace belongs to exactly one space: its owner's personal space, unless the owner placed it in a team space. The space's own member list is the only authority on who belongs to it and in what role; the owner's record of a workspace is the only authority on which space it belongs to. The kernel side is `packages/workshop-backend/src/spaces.ts`, the "Spaces" and "Workspaces in spaces" parts of `user.ts` and the "Space roles" part of `overseer.ts`; the API is listed under "API surface" below.

Belonging to a space decides where a workspace is listed, who sees it listed, the address it has there (see "Addresses") and who may open it as a member: a member's role applies to every workspace the space lists (see "Access through membership"). The workspace's own sharing applies alongside, and a user opens in the higher of the two roles (see docs/sharing.md). A workspace that holds restricted data or is owner-invites-only still belongs to a space, but no space lists it and membership gives nothing on it (see "Workspaces a space never lists").

## The model

There are two kinds of space (`SpaceKind`), fixed when the space is created. Spaces form a flat list: a space has no parent and contains no other space.

| | Personal space | Team space |
|---|---|---|
| Created | For each user, the first time it is needed: by `listSpaces()`, or by a workspace of theirs that belongs in it | By any signed-in user, with `createSpace(key, name)` |
| Key | `~` followed by a part the server chooses, e.g. `~alice` or `~alice-2` | Chosen by the creator |
| Name | The owner's display name when the space was created | Given by the creator: trimmed, then 1 to 100 characters |
| Admins | The owner, and nobody else | One or more of its members |
| Other members | Allowed, as `build` or `use` | Allowed, in any role |
| Adds workspaces | The owner, and nobody else | Any member, whatever their role |

### Keys

A **team key** matches `TEAM_SPACE_KEY_PATTERN`, `[a-z0-9][a-z0-9-]{1,31}`: 2 to 32 lowercase ASCII letters, digits and dashes, the first not a dash. No key that matches is reserved. A **personal key** is `PERSONAL_SPACE_PREFIX` (`~`) followed by a string of the same grammar. A team key cannot start with `~`, so the two kinds never collide. `isValidTeamSpaceKey()` and `isValidSpaceKey()` are the checks `createSpace` and `openSpace` apply, and the first is also what `newGadget` and `moveToSpace` hold a `spaceKey` to; they are exported so a client refuses the same keys. A key is unique and immutable, and it is never released: nothing renames or deletes a space.

### Members and roles

Every member holds exactly one `SpaceMemberRole`, and being a member in any role is what lets a user open the space:

- **`admin`** -- may also change the member list (`Space.setMemberRole`, `Space.removeMember`) and the address of any workspace the space lists (`Space.setWorkspaceSlug`, see "Addresses"). On the workspaces themselves an admin holds `build`, and no more.
- **`build`** and **`use`** -- ordinary members, named after the `CollaboratorRole` level (see docs/sharing.md) each holds on the workspaces the space lists. In the space itself the two confer the same thing: reading its info, its member list and its listing of workspaces, resolving an address in it, adding workspaces of their own to a team space, changing the address of a workspace of their own that it lists, and leaving it.

`setMemberRole(username, role)` sets a role *exactly*: it adds a non-member, and raises or lowers an existing member, who keeps their `added` date. `removeMember(profileId)` lets an admin remove anyone and any other member remove only themself, which is how one leaves a space; an admin removing someone who is not a member does nothing. Removing a member, or lowering one to `use`, also ends the sessions they hold through the role they had (see "Leases and revocations").

A refused open does not say whether the space exists: a key nobody has claimed and a space the caller does not belong to are refused with one error (`noSuchSpace()`).

### Invariants

`SpaceModel` holds the rules of a space, for its members and for its listing, as pure logic over typed storage, with no RPC, so they are unit-tested directly (`packages/workshop-backend/__tests__/spaces.test.ts`). `SpaceDurableObject` is a thin shell around it that adds the account lookup, the mirror pushes and the alarm that delivers revocations.

- **A space always has at least one admin.** Demoting or removing its last admin throws.
- **A personal space's owner is its only admin.** Nobody else can be made admin, and so, by the rule above, the owner can be neither demoted nor removed.

## Where the state lives

**One Durable Object per space.** Each space is a `SpaceDurableObject`, addressed by `getByName(spaceKey)`. Its storage (`storage-schema/space-storage.ts`) is a singleton `info` -- the key, name, kind and, for a personal space, the owner; absent until the key is claimed and never removed -- a collection `members` keyed by profile id, a collection `workspaces` keyed by workspace id (the listing, see "Workspaces in a space"), and two collections behind the roles its members hold on those workspaces, `leases` and `revocations`, with the counter `nextRevocation` (see "Leases and revocations"). A personal space's owner is stored in `members` too, as its only admin.

**No directory.** No object lists the spaces or the keys in use. A key is taken once the object under it has been claimed, and whether it has is asked of that object.

**The user's own Durable Object** holds, besides its records of the user's workspaces (see "Workspaces in a space"), two things (`storage-schema/user-storage.ts`). The second authorizes nothing, and the first only completes a record's pointer:

- `personalSpaceKey` -- the key of the user's personal space, once one has been claimed for them. It says which space to list first, and which space the user's workspaces that are in no team space belong to: the space that lists them, and the one asked which role a member holds on them.
- `spaces` -- a **mirror** of the user's memberships, one `SpaceInfo` per space with `role` set to that user's role. Once `personalSpaceKey` is set, `listSpaces()` is answered from the user's own object alone, waiting on no call to any space.

### Leases and revocations

The space answers one question about its workspaces for the rest of the kernel: which role a profile holds on one, as a member (`SpaceModel.workspaceRole(id, ownerId, profileId)`; see "Access through membership"). What it has answered, it has to be able to take back, so it keeps two more collections.

- `leases` -- one `SpaceLease`, a (workspace, profile) pair, for every answer that gave a role and has not been taken back since: the sessions a change to the members or to the listing may have to end. An answer of no role leaves none. Keyed `<workspace>:<profile>`, so one workspace's leases are a key prefix, and indexed by profile.
- `revocations` -- the leases taken back and not yet delivered, each a `SpaceRevocation`: the lease, a `seq` from `nextRevocation` that is never reused, `due`, the time of its next attempt, and `retryMs`, how long it last waited for one. A new revocation has both at 0, which makes it due at once. The index `byDue` is the order they are attempted in: by when each is due and, among those due together, as queued.

**What takes a lease back.** `removeMember` revokes every lease of that member; `setMemberRole`, those of a member lowered from a role that gave `build` (`admin` or `build`) to `use`; `detachWorkspace`, every lease on that workspace, whatever took it off the listing: a move, a delete, or a flag that means no space may list it. A member raised, or moved between `admin` and `build`, loses nothing. The lease is deleted and its revocation queued in the same turn as the change, and the alarm is set for now in that turn too, so the three are stored together. A role given again before the revocation is delivered is a new lease; the revocation still goes out.

**Delivery.** `SpaceDurableObject.alarm()` takes up to 16 of the queued revocations that are due, the longest due first, and for each calls `OverseerDurableObject.revokeSpaceAccess(profile)` on the workspace's Overseer, which, if it gave that profile a role, forgets it and restarts the workspace (docs/sharing.md, "Space roles"). A revocation leaves the queue only once its call has returned. One whose call fails is logged (`space.revocation.deliver.failed`) and kept, due again after a wait of its own that doubles with each failure from one second to five minutes. That wait is stored with the revocation, so it does not start over when the space's object is discarded from memory, and it holds up no other: a revocation queued later is due at once and attempted in the next run, however many are waiting to be retried. While any remain the alarm is set for when the next is due, which is now while any is. No Overseer is ever polled: a workspace hears from its space only when a role the space gave out on it has ended.

### The mirror

The mirror is presentation only, like the record a recipient's account keeps of a workspace shared with them (`UserDurableObject.recordSharedGadgetOpen()`, see docs/sharing.md). Every operation on a space is decided from what the space itself stores at the time of the call -- its `info`, its member list and, for an entry of its listing, the owner that entry is listed under (see "Workspaces in a space"); nothing is ever authorized from the mirror.

The space keeps it current by pushing to the affected user's object -- `recordSpaceMembership(info)` after `setMemberRole`, `forgetSpace(key)` after `removeMember`. The push is best-effort: the member list is already written, so a push that fails is logged (`space.membership.mirror.failed`) and the change stands. Opening is what heals a lost push: before `SpaceDurableObject.open` answers, the space makes the same push for the caller, recording their membership if they have one and dropping the key if they do not. So a listed space may still refuse to open, and a membership the mirror missed appears once that space has been opened by key.

## Claiming a key

`SpaceModel.claim(claim, creator)` creates the space: in one transaction it writes `info` and makes `creator` the first admin and, for a personal space, the owner, so a claim that fails leaves the key unclaimed. It returns whether `creator` now holds the key in the way they asked for.

- **The first claim wins.** The Durable Object serializes claims, so two users claiming one key at once get exactly one `true`.
- **One idempotent case.** The owner of a personal space claiming it again as their personal space gets `true`, and nothing changes. That is what lets a personal allocation resume (below).
- **A claim is never a takeover.** Every other claim of a claimed key returns `false` and changes nothing. Since the object under a key is the space, a claim that could rewrite it would hand the space and its member list to whoever asked.

`createSpace(key, name)` makes the claim for a team space and refuses a `false` as "already exists" -- also when the earlier claimant was the caller, and so disclosing that the key is taken. It then opens the space, and that open is what records it in the creator's mirror.

## Personal space allocation

`listSpaces()` first ensures the caller's personal space (`UserDurableObject.#ensurePersonalSpace()`), as does anything that needs the space a workspace with no `spaceKey` belongs to. If `personalSpaceKey` is set there is nothing to do. Otherwise the user's object claims the candidates of `personalSpaceClaim(owner, attempt)` in order -- `~base`, `~base-2`, `~base-3`, ... -- until a space grants one, then remembers that key and writes the mirror record.

The base is the local part of the profile id (what precedes any `@`), reduced to the key alphabet and cut so that the suffix still fits the grammar. Two users whose ids share a local part therefore get `~name` and `~name-2`, in the order their personal spaces are first needed. This is why the derivation is not part of the shared API: a client reads the key from the first entry of `listSpaces()` and never derives it from a user id.

- **Resumed, not restarted.** The key is remembered only once it has been granted. If the object stops between the claim and remembering it, the next allocation tries the same candidates in the same order, and the personal space grants its owner's claim again, so the user ends up on the key they had already claimed instead of stranding it and taking the next one.
- **Serialized.** Concurrent callers share one in-flight allocation, so they allocate one key between them.

## Workspaces in a space

Where a workspace belongs is recorded in two places, and the first is the authority.

- **The pointer** is `spaceKey` on the owner's record of the workspace in their User DO (`GadgetRecord`, which extends `GadgetMetadata`). It is only ever a team key; absent means the owner's personal space. The Overseer stores nothing about spaces, and a user's record of a workspace shared with them never carries a pointer.
- **The listing** is the space's `workspaces` collection: each workspace's id, title, a snapshot of its owner's profile, when it was created and, once it has one, its slug (`SpaceWorkspaceInfo`; see "Addresses"). `Space.listWorkspaces()` returns it to any member, newest first. It holds only workspaces that have seen activity and that a space may list (both below).

The pointer is the authority because the owner's record is already where the kernel keeps everything else a listing shows or depends on -- that the workspace exists, its title, when it was created, whether it has seen activity, whether a space may list it -- and is what a delete removes. So a space is told where a workspace belongs and is never asked. The listing follows the pointer and can lag it (below), so where a workspace belongs is never decided from the listing. Whether a member may open it takes both: the pointer has to name the space and the space has to list the workspace under that owner (see "Access through membership"), so a listing that lags gives a role to nobody the pointer does not. Beyond that an entry decides only its own upkeep: it is updated or dropped only by the owner it is listed under, who while still a member of the space is, along with its admins, also who may change its slug.

**Who may add.** `SpaceModel.canAddWorkspaces(profileId)`: only the owner of a personal space; any member of a team space, `use` included. `attachWorkspaces(owner, registrations)` is an upsert that returns `false`, having changed nothing, if it refuses any registration. A workspace not yet listed needs `canAddWorkspaces(owner.id)`. One listed under the same owner is updated whether or not they may still add, which is how a workspace keeps its place, and a current title, after its owner leaves the space. One listed under another owner is refused. `detachWorkspace(id, ownerId)` removes an entry only if that owner holds it, and with it revokes the leases on that workspace.

### Registration and the reconcile

A workspace that a space may list (next section) registers with its space at its first activity, never when its record is created. `AuthenticatedApi.newGadget` creates a workspace *provisional*, before the user has sent anything, and it may never be used, so a record with no `lastActive` is listed nowhere. For `newGadgetFromBlueprint` the first activity is part of the creation: instantiating the blueprint marks the workspace active, which starts its registration. Both check only that a `spaceKey` is a well-formed team key and store the pointer.

`registered` on the record is the marker: the `{ spaceKey, title }` a space last acknowledged, where `spaceKey` is that space's real key, personal or team. A marker with no `title` names a space that has acknowledged nothing: it was asked to list the workspace, or to drop it, and may or may not have. A marker is written before any space is first asked to list the workspace, so a record with no marker is on no listing. It is the User DO's own bookkeeping: `listGadgets()` and `getGadget()` strip it.

`UserDurableObject.#reconcileSpace(id)` is what brings a listing in line with its record; the only other things the owner's User DO writes to a listing with are the backfill's pages and a delete (both below). One write comes from elsewhere, a member's change of slug (`Space.setWorkspaceSlug`, see "Addresses"): it changes the slug and former slugs of the entry and, where it takes a former slug over, the former slugs of the entry that held it. Its target is the space the record points at, or no space for a workspace that none may list (next section). It does nothing for a provisional workspace, for one shared with this user, or for one listed as it should be: the marker names the target with the record's current title or, with no target, there is no marker. Otherwise it **attaches** the workspace to the target, having first written a marker without a title if the record had none; then **detaches** it from every other space that may list it, which is the space the marker named and, when there is no target, also the space the record points at; and only then **writes the marker**, or with no target removes it.

A failure leaves the marker out of step, so the next reconcile does whatever is left. A refused attach has changed nothing: a marker written just before it is removed again. An attach or a detach that fails without an answer may or may not have been applied by the space; the next reconcile starts again from the attach, which is safe because the attach is an upsert and the detach does nothing for an entry already gone. Until then a workspace on its way from one space to another may be listed in both; because the attach comes first, no failed reconcile takes a workspace that a space may list off the one listing it was on. Everything that touches a listing runs on one chain per User DO (`#inSpaceOrder()`), so reconciles never interleave, whichever workspaces they are for.

`#syncSpace(id)` is the reconcile plus the fallback below. `setGadgetLastActive` (the first activity and every later one; a later one finds nothing to do unless the workspace may no longer be listed, or a sync, a move or a delete that failed left it out of step, which makes the next activity the usual retry) and `updateTitle` run it detached, logging a failure as `space.workspace.sync.failed`, so that it neither slows nor fails the Overseer's call. The move and the delete await it, and the backfill runs it.

### Workspaces a space never lists

A listing shows a workspace's title to every member of the space, and a title can derive from what the workspace has observed. So a workspace that holds restricted data (docs/observers.md) or is owner-invites-only (docs/sharing.md, "`ownerInvitesOnly`") is on no listing. Nor does membership give a role on it.

- **The rule.** A space lists a workspace only while `containsRestrictedData` and `ownerInvitesOnly` are both known to be false on its owner's record (`isListable()`). A flag that is set and a flag that was never reported keep the workspace out alike: unknown counts as hidden.
- **Where the flags come from.** Both are permanent, one-way flags in the Overseer's storage, set by `authorizeObservation`. The Overseer states them (`OverseerImpl.restrictions`) with each activity report (`setGadgetLastActive`), each title update (`updateTitle`), a move (`setGadgetSpace`) and a delete (`deleteGadget`), which are all of its calls that can lead to a listing write, and the User DO records them on the owner's record before it syncs (`mirrorRestrictions()`), in the two `GadgetMetadata` fields: `false`, `true`, or absent while the Overseer has never reported. A title update writes the title and the flags it states in one step, so a title written after a flag was set arrives with that flag and reaches no listing. The one other thing that leads to a listing write is not a call of the Overseer's and goes by the record's flags: the backfill, which `listSpaces()` starts and which asks only for flags that are unknown. A flag recorded as set stays set, whatever a report that arrives later states, since reports can arrive out of order.
- **A prompt report.** When an observation first sets either flag, the Overseer sends its activity report at once, not at the next throttled one, and neither waits for it nor fails with it. If that report is lost (`gadget.last.active.bump.failed`), the next activity report, title update, move or delete states the flags anyway, and until then the record says what it said before (see "Known limitations").
- **What the reconcile does.** Its target for such a workspace is no space: every space that may list it drops it and the marker is removed, so the report that says a listed workspace has become restricted is what takes it off its listing. The pointer is kept. It still says where the owner grouped the workspace, and `listGadgets()` returns it to the owner along with the two flags; the owner's own list of workspaces is read from their records, not from a listing, so the workspace stays there. The flags never clear, so the workspace is never listed again.
- **No role through membership.** The Overseer of such a workspace gives no member a role, deciding from its own two flags and asking nobody, and when an observation first sets either it ends at once the roles it had given, restarting the workspace if it had given any. That does not wait for the report, the reconcile or the space. The owner's User DO refuses separately: it asks no space about a workspace whose record does not say both flags are false. And when the reconcile drops the entry, the space revokes the leases on it (see "Leases and revocations").
- **A move** records the flags the Overseer states with it and syncs the workspace where it is, which takes one that was still on a listing off it. After that it only changes the pointer, as for a provisional workspace: nothing is attached and the target is not asked whether the owner may add to it, so the move does not throw `noSuchSpace()`. A malformed key still throws, before anything is recorded. Nor does a sync of such a workspace ever fall back, so its pointer may name a team space its owner cannot add to.

### A refused registration, a refused move

**A sync falls back.** When a space refuses the attach during `#syncSpace` -- the owner may not add to the team space the record names, and the workspace is not already listed there -- the record is pointed back at the space that still lists the workspace or, if no other space does, at the owner's personal space, and reconciled again; `space.workspace.fallback` is logged at `info`. So a workspace created in a team space its owner may not add to ends up in their personal space, with no error.

**A move refuses.** `Overseer.moveToSpace(spaceKey)` is for the workspace's owner only and calls their `UserDurableObject.setGadgetSpace(id, spaceKey, restrictions)`; `null` means the personal space. That records the flags the Overseer states, syncs the workspace where it is, then points the record at the target and reconciles. If the target refuses, the record is pointed back where it was and the call throws `noSuchSpace()`, the refusal an open gives, so the move has changed nothing and the answer does not say whether the space exists; the sync before it may have changed something all the same, for example by finishing an earlier move or falling back from one. If a call that reconcile makes to a space fails without an answer it may still have reached the space, so the record stays pointed at the target, and the next sync either finishes the move or, on a refusal, falls back to where the workspace is listed. For a provisional workspace a move only changes the pointer. For one that no space may list, going by the flags the Overseer states with the move, the sync takes it off any listing it was still on, and the move then only changes the pointer (see "Workspaces a space never lists").

### Delete

`UserDurableObject.deleteGadget(id, restrictions)` first records the flags the Overseer states with it, then syncs one of the user's own workspaces that has seen activity, which leaves the space the marker names as the only one that lists it, or for a workspace that no space may list, leaves no marker and no listing. That sync may list a workspace that was out of step, which is why the delete states the flags: one whose Overseer holds a flag is not listed on its way out, even if every earlier report of the flag was lost. It then takes the title off the marker, detaches the workspace from the space the marker names, and only then drops the record; with no marker there is nothing to detach. If the sync or the detach fails, the delete fails and the record is kept, because once it is gone nothing would ever remove the entry. `Overseer.deleteSelf()` makes this call before it destroys the workspace's storage, so that storage is intact and its owner can delete the workspace again; by then its running agents have been cancelled and its enabled hooks disabled, and the hooks stay disabled. The marker loses its title before the detach, so a record that outlives a failed delete is out of step, and its next sync lists the workspace again, or drops it if by then no space may list it. The sync comes first so that the marker left without a title names the very space the record points at: a refused sync falls back to the space the marker names only when that is another space, taking the marker's word that it still lists the workspace.

### Backfill

`listSpaces()` starts `#backfillSpaces()` without waiting for it, once per Durable Object instance and again after one that failed (`space.workspace.backfill.failed`). It brings the listing of every workspace of the user's own that has seen activity in line with its record, in three steps.

1. **Learn the flags that are unknown.** `#backfillRestrictions()` takes each of those workspaces whose record lacks a flag, which is one whose Overseer has never reported, and asks that Overseer: `OverseerDurableObject.getRestrictionsForOwnerBackfill(ownerId)` answers with both flags, or with null unless the caller is the workspace's owner. This wakes the Overseers asked, 16 at a time, each page settled with `Promise.allSettled` before the next starts. An answer is recorded on the record as a report is. A workspace whose Overseer fails or answers null stays unknown, so hidden, and does not hold up the others: the failures are counted and logged (`space.workspace.backfill.partial`) and the backfill goes on. The one exception is a page on which every call failed, which looks like an outage: as in `#backfillOutputs()`, no further page is asked in that backfill, and steps 2 and 3 still run. Each workspace is asked at most once per backfill and never again once its flags are known; one left unknown, asked or not, is settled by its next activity report, title update or move, or asked by a later backfill, which starts again from the first of them.
2. **Register with the personal space.** The workspaces that may be listed, have no pointer and are not yet acknowledged as they are now, which includes every listable one older than the listing, go to the personal space in pages of up to 128 per `attachWorkspaces` call. Each that has no marker gets one without a title before the call, and each page is marked once acknowledged, so a backfill that stops resumes with what is left. A workspace whose marker names another space is left to the next step, since that space has to drop it.
3. **Sync the rest.** Every other record out of step goes through `#syncSpace`. That makes the backfill the retry for a sync that failed earlier on a workspace that has seen no activity since, and it is what takes a workspace off a listing it is on while its record says it may not be listed, its flags unknown included. Like step 2 it goes by the record, and step 1 asks only where a flag is unknown: a workspace whose record still says both flags are false, the report of a newly set one having been lost, is treated in both steps as one a space may list (see "Known limitations").

## Addresses

A **slug** is a workspace's address within its space (`SpaceWorkspaceInfo.slug`), unique within that space. It belongs to the workspace's entry in the listing and is stored nowhere else: the owner's User DO and the Overseer know nothing of slugs, and a registration (`WorkspaceRegistration`) carries none, because the slug is the space's to give. The stored entry (`SpaceWorkspaceRecord`) also keeps the slugs it used to have, `formerSlugs`, oldest first, which are never sent to a client; two indexes of the `workspaces` collection, `bySlug` (unique) and `byFormerSlug`, are how a slug is looked up. The Workshop addresses a workspace as `/spaces/<key>/<slug>`, and `/workspace/<id>` keeps working. The rules are `SpaceModel`'s, tested in `packages/workshop-backend/__tests__/spaces-slugs.test.ts`.

- **Minted once.** `attachWorkspaces` gives an entry that has no slug one the first time it writes that entry with a title that is not a placeholder (`PLACEHOLDER_TITLES`, storage-schema/overseer-storage.ts): `DEFAULT_WORKSPACE_TITLE`, the "Untitled Workspace" `newGadget` creates a workspace with, or "Untitled Gadget", which the records of older workspaces still carry for the same reason. A placeholder says nothing about the workspace, so an entry listed under one has no slug until a sync brings another title or someone sets one (see "Renaming"). An entry that has a slug keeps it, and its former slugs, through every later update, so a change of title never moves an address.
- **The derived slug** is `slugify(title)` (api.ts): lowercased, accents stripped, every run of characters other than the ASCII letters `a`-`z` and the digits collapsed to one dash, trimmed of dashes and cut to `MAX_SLUG_LENGTH` (80), or "workspace" when nothing remains, as for a title with no such letter or digit (one written wholly in another script, for instance). When that is taken, it is that slug ending in the first of `-2`, `-3`, ... that is not, cut first where the suffix would not fit, so every slug a space gives is within `MAX_SLUG_LENGTH` and in slug form. For a derived slug, *taken* means that some entry has it now or holds it as a former slug: a former slug still leads to the workspace that gave it up, so a derived slug never takes an old link over.
- **Renaming.** `Space.setWorkspaceSlug(id, slug)` is the only thing that changes a slug, and it also gives one to an entry that has none. The caller must be a member of the space and either the owner the entry is listed under or an admin of the space, so an owner who has left the space cannot rename. `slug` must be in slug form, `slugify(slug) === slug`, which rules out the empty string too. Asking for the slug the entry already has changes nothing.
- **Former slugs.** A rename keeps the slug the entry had as a former slug, which goes on resolving to it. An entry keeps its 32 most recent (`MAX_FORMER_SLUGS`); an older one stops resolving and is free again. Asking for one of the entry's own former slugs makes that one current again.
- **Taking a former slug over.** A slug asked for by name is refused only while another entry has it *now*. One that another entry only used to have is taken: it leaves that entry's former slugs and stops resolving to it. Whoever asks for a slug by name knows the address they want, which a derivation does not.
- **Resolving.** `Space.resolveWorkspace(slug)` answers any member, as the listing does; anyone else gets the refusal an open gives (`noSuchSpace()`). It returns the entry as the listing shows it, with `canonical` true when `slug` is the entry's current slug and false when it is one of its former slugs, in which case a client replaces its URL with `workspace.slug`. It returns null when no entry has or had the slug.
- **Leaving a listing gives the address up.** A slug and the former slugs go with their entry, and are free at once. So a workspace that was deleted, moved to another space, or stopped being listable (see "Workspaces a space never lists") no longer resolves in that space under any slug it had, and nothing leads from there to where it went: there is no redirect across spaces. A workspace that comes onto a listing gets a new entry, also in a space it was in before, and with it a slug by the minting rule, from its title at that time.
- **Nothing is authorized from a slug.** Resolving one returns what the listing already shows a member and grants nothing of its own. Opening it is `openGadget(workspace.id)`, which a member may do in the role their membership gives them (see "Access through membership"). A member may still resolve a slug to a workspace that refuses them: one whose record no longer points at this space, such as a workspace on its way to another, or one whose connections they cannot be verified against.

## Access through membership

A member's role applies to every workspace the space lists: `admin` and `build` open it to build, `use` to use it, with no share from the workspace's owner. How that role is combined with the workspace's own sharing, verified, kept from granting anything to others and revoked is docs/sharing.md, "Space roles"; this section says what the space and the owner's User DO contribute. The tests of both halves are `packages/workshop-backend/__tests__/spaces-roles.test.ts`.

- **Both sides have to agree.** The workspace's Overseer asks its owner's User DO (`UserDurableObject.workspaceRoleInSpace(id, profileId)`), which answers only for a workspace of the user's own that has seen activity and that a space may list (see "Workspaces a space never lists"), and then asks one space: the one the record points at, the personal space when it has no `spaceKey`. That space gives a role only to a member, and only for a workspace it lists under that owner (`SpaceModel.workspaceRole`). So a provisional workspace, one no space may list, one whose pointer names a space that does not list it yet, and one listed by a space the pointer has moved away from all give no role. An unallocated personal space lists nothing, and the lookup does not allocate one.
- **The owner is not affected.** A workspace keeps its individual owner, in a team space too and after that person leaves the space. Only the owner moves or deletes it, and membership gives nobody the power to share it.
- **A role given is a lease.** Each answer that gives a role is remembered, so that removing or lowering the member, or the workspace leaving the listing, reaches the sessions already open (see "Leases and revocations").
- **Reached through the space.** A workspace a member opens through their role alone is not added to their own list of workspaces (`listGadgets()`), and the member is not among its collaborators: the space's listing is where they find it.

## Trust boundary

Every method `SpaceDurableObject` exposes takes the acting user as a plain parameter -- their profile id, or for `claim` and `attachWorkspaces` their profile -- like `OverseerDurableObject.open()`. Its only callers are `AuthenticatedApiImpl` (server.ts), which passes the identity of the authenticated session, and `UserDurableObject` (user.ts), which passes its own -- never a client, gadget, gatekeeper or agent -- so the parameter is authoritative.

`attachWorkspaces`, `detachWorkspace` and `workspaceRole` are called by `UserDurableObject` alone, which states its own user as the owner, so a user's workspaces are only ever registered or dropped by that user's object, and a space is only ever asked for a role on a workspace by the object whose record places it there. None is on `Space`. `UserDurableObject.workspaceRoleInSpace` in turn is called only by a workspace's Overseer, about itself, and `OverseerDurableObject.revokeSpaceAccess` only by a space's alarm; all it can do is end sessions, which then reopen and are authorized afresh. The two flags that decide whether a workspace may be listed reach that object only from the workspace's own Overseer, in calls no client can make.

A client only ever holds a `Space`: the `SpaceClientInterface` minted by `SpaceDurableObject.open()`, which closes over the caller fixed at open time. It carries no standing permission. Every method has the space look that user's membership up again, so a stub held by someone who has since been removed or demoted loses those powers at once, with no session to terminate. A workspace they have open is another matter: that session was authorized when it opened, and is what a revocation ends.

`setMemberRole` checks that the caller is an admin *before* it resolves the username, so a non-admin cannot use it to find out which accounts exist. The lookup is the one `Overseer.addCollaborator` uses (`UserDurableObject.whoamiIfExists()`), and likewise returns null for a username with no account.

## API surface

- `AuthenticatedApi.listSpaces()` -- the caller's spaces from their mirror: their personal space first, then the others by name.
- `AuthenticatedApi.openSpace(key)` -- a `Space` acting as the caller. A malformed key throws; a well-formed one the caller cannot open gets the single refusal above.
- `AuthenticatedApi.createSpace(key, name)` -- claims a team key and opens the new space, with the caller as its first admin.
- `Space` -- `getInfo()`, `listMembers()` and `listWorkspaces()` (which never shows a workspace that holds restricted data or is owner-invites-only) for any member; `setMemberRole(username, role)` for admins; `removeMember(profileId)` for an admin, or a member removing themself. The last two also change who may open the workspaces the space lists.
- `AuthenticatedApi.openGadget(id)` -- opens, besides a workspace the caller owns or one shared with them, one that belongs to a space they are a member of and that the space lists (see "Access through membership"). `GadgetMetadata.role` on the session is the higher of the role that membership gives and the one the workspace's sharing does, and does not say which it came from.
- `Space.resolveWorkspace(slug)` -- for any member: the listed workspace a slug addresses and whether the slug is its current one (`SpaceWorkspaceResolution`), or null. `Space.setWorkspaceSlug(id, slug)` -- for a member who is the workspace's owner as the listing records them, or an admin: changes the workspace's slug and returns its entry.
- `SpaceWorkspaceInfo.slug` -- a listed workspace's address within the space; absent while it has none. `slugify(title)` and `MAX_SLUG_LENGTH` are exported so a client can preview the slug a title leads to and refuse what `setWorkspaceSlug` would.
- `AuthenticatedApi.newGadget(spaceKey?)` and `newGadgetFromBlueprint(blueprintId, bindings, spaceKey?)` -- create the workspace in a team space; omitted, in the caller's personal space. A malformed key throws.
- `Overseer.moveToSpace(spaceKey | null)` -- owner only: moves the workspace to a team space, or with `null` back to its owner's personal space.
- `GadgetMetadata.spaceKey` -- the team space a workspace belongs to, on its owner's own record only (so in `listGadgets()` for a workspace the caller owns); absent means the owner's personal space.
- `GadgetMetadata.containsRestrictedData` and `ownerInvitesOnly` -- in `listGadgets()` for a workspace the caller owns, the two flags as the workspace last reported them; absent there means not yet known, not false.

## Known limitations

- **Display names are snapshots.** The display name in a member record, in `SpaceInfo.owner`, in a personal space's `name` and in a listed workspace's `owner` may trail a rename. A listed workspace's title trails a change until its next sync.
- **A personal space's members get every workspace it lists.** A personal space is where its owner's workspaces belong unless placed in a team space, so a member added to it holds their role on each of those that has seen activity, the ones created later included. There is no choosing among them: a single workspace is shared through its own sharing (docs/sharing.md).
- **Ending a role restarts the whole workspace.** A revocation disconnects every session of the workspace, the owner's included, not only the affected member's. It does so whenever the workspace's Overseer, since it last started, gave that member a role, whether or not anything of theirs is still open: it may have learned the role while checking an observation that excludes them, and it cannot tell that a client kept nothing a closed session handed out. Each client reopens and is authorized afresh.
- **A session outlives its role for a moment.** Between the change in the space and the alarm delivering the revocation, a session already open continues, and longer while the workspace's Overseer cannot be reached: the revocation is retried, with waits of up to five minutes, for as long as it takes. A workspace on its way to another space keeps the sessions opened through the space it is leaving until that space drops its entry, which a failed detach puts off to the next sync. Opens are not subject to this window, since each asks the space as it stands.
- **Leases are only ever revoked.** A lease stays until its member is removed or lowered or its workspace leaves the listing, whether or not the session it was given for is still open, so a workspace leaving a listing queues one revocation for every lease still standing on it: one for each member answered with a role on it since their leases were last revoked. (A member lowered to `use` had theirs revoked then, and holds one again only if answered with a role afterwards.) Its Overseer ignores each one for a member it gave no role since it last started and is not asking about.
- **A late revocation restarts a workspace whose member still has a role.** A revocation that arrives after its member regained a role by another route, for example on a workspace moved between two spaces they belong to and reopened before the space it left was heard from, finds that role remembered and restarts the workspace once; the member reopens in the role they had.
- **A member can be turned away once while roles are being revoked.** At an open, a lookup that revocations of other members' roles overtake twice in a row counts as no space role (docs/sharing.md, "Lookups in flight"), so in that rare interleaving a member with no role from the workspace's own sharing is refused, and opens on trying again.
- **A workspace does not show who reaches it through its space.** Its collaborator list holds only the people its own sharing admits, and a session does not say whether its role came from the space, so a member who builds through the space and tries to share finds out from the refusal.
- **A listing shows a workspace to members before they can open it.** Every member of a space sees the title, owner, creation date and slug of each workspace it lists, including a member who has not passed, or cannot pass, the verification that opening it takes (docs/observers.md). The title shown may be one a model generated from the workspace's chat, and the slug, which derives from the first title that was not a placeholder, goes on showing that title after it changes.
- **A workspace that becomes restricted stays listed for a moment.** Between the observation that sets a flag and the owner's User DO acting on the report of it, the entry already on a listing stays visible, with the title it had before. It gives no member a role in that time: the workspace's Overseer ends those itself, with the write that sets the flag. Two failures make that last longer. If the prompt report is lost, the owner's record still says the workspace may be listed, until the next call from its Overseer states the flags: its next activity report, title update, move or delete. Until then an entry it has stays, and a backfill does not end that, since it asks only the Overseers of workspaces whose flags are unknown: if the record is out of step, the backfill's page for the personal space or its retry of the sync even gives the workspace an entry, or brings the one it has up to date, with the title as the record has it, which predates the flag. If the report arrives but the space cannot be reached to drop the entry, the record is out of step, and each later sync tries again until one drops the entry: one runs at the workspace's next activity report, title update, move or delete, and in its owner's next backfill.
- **A workspace whose flags are unknown is hidden.** One the backfill got no answer for is on no listing, and gives no member a role, until its Overseer reports or answers a later backfill, though it may hold no restricted data and have no sharing restriction.
- **A workspace cannot be evicted.** It stays in a team space after its owner leaves or is removed, and only its owner can move or delete it: a space admin cannot take it off the listing. The space's members keep their roles on it meanwhile, and its owner, no longer a member, still owns it.
- **Membership is checked at registration, not at creation.** `newGadget(spaceKey)` and `newGadgetFromBlueprint(…, spaceKey)` accept any well-formed team key, and a workspace whose owner may not add to that space falls back to their personal space at its first activity (for one made from a blueprint, as it is created), with nothing reported to the caller. A workspace that no space may list never registers, so its pointer is never checked: it may name a team space its owner does not belong to, or a key nobody has claimed.
- **A workspace can be listed by two spaces.** During a move both list it between the attach and the detach, and when either call fails, possibly until the next sync. Until then it resolves in both, though only the members of the space its record points at can open it through their membership.
- **A workspace whose title never left the placeholder has no address.** It has no slug until it is given another title or someone sets a slug, and until then is reached by its id alone. Only the placeholders are skipped: any other title, however generic, derives a slug.
- **A slug comes from the first title a space records that is not a placeholder, whatever chose it.** A workspace made from a blueprint is created with the blueprint's title, so its slug derives from that (`<blueprint-title>`, `<blueprint-title>-2`, ...), and one created under the placeholder usually derives its slug from the title a model generated from its first chat. Either stays through later titles until someone changes the slug.
- **An entry listed before it could be given a slug is not given one until it is next written.** Minting happens when an entry is written, and the reconcile writes one only when its record is out of step, so such an entry, stored under a title of its own, has no slug until its title next changes or someone sets its address.
- **An address does not survive a move.** A workspace moved to another space, or back, loses the slugs it had, and links to them stop resolving or come to resolve to another workspace that took the slug since. The same holds, for good, for a workspace that comes to hold restricted data or becomes owner-invites-only. What stays is the workspace's id.
- **The listing is not paginated.** `listWorkspaces()` returns every workspace of the space in one answer.
- **A lost mirror write hides a space until it is opened.** In particular, a team space whose creator's mirror write was lost is absent from their list until they open it by key.
- **Personal keys are probed in order.** Allocation claims `~base`, `~base-2`, ... one call at a time with no bound, so a user's allocation makes a call for every candidate already taken before the one it is granted.
