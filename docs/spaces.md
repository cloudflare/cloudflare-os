# Spaces

A space groups users and workspaces: it is an id, a display name, a list of members and a listing of the workspaces that belong to it. Every user has one **personal space**, and any signed-in user may create **team spaces**. Every workspace belongs to exactly one space: its owner's personal space, unless the owner placed it in a team space. The space's own member list is the only authority on who belongs to it and in what role; the owner's record of a workspace is the only authority on which space it belongs to. The kernel side is `packages/workshop-backend/src/spaces.ts`, the "Spaces" and "Workspaces in spaces" parts of `user.ts` and the "Space roles" part of `overseer.ts`; the API is listed under "API surface" below. The Workshop's UI for spaces is behind the `spaces` UI flag.

Belonging to a space decides where a workspace is listed, who sees it listed and who may open it as a member: a member's role applies to every workspace the space lists (see "Access through membership"). The workspace's own sharing applies alongside, and a user opens with what either gives them (see docs/sharing.md, "Space roles"). A workspace that holds restricted data or is owner-invites-only is listed and opened like any other (see "Access through membership").

## Ids

A space is addressed by its id and by nothing else. The id is the id of the space's Durable Object (`SpaceDurableObject`), a string of 64 hex digits that clients treat as opaque (`SpaceInfo.id`). It is unique and immutable. The Workshop's page for a space is `/spaces/<id>`; a workspace's stays `/workspace/<id>`.

- **Where an id comes from.** A team space's id is made by the server when the space is created (`SpaceDurableObject.newUniqueId()` in `AuthenticatedApiImpl.createSpace`); a caller never supplies it. A personal space's is derived from its owner (`SpaceDurableObject.idFromName(profile.id)`), so it is the same on every call and nothing is stored to find it. The object takes its id from `ctx.id`: no caller tells a space its id, and the space does not store it.
- **An id is not a capability.** Every call has the space look the acting user's membership up at that moment, so knowing an id opens nothing. An id that names no space, a space the caller is not a member of, and another user's personal space are all refused with one error, `noSuchSpace()` ("No such space, or you are not a member of it."), so the answer does not say whether the space exists.
- **A string that is not an id is refused before it is recorded or addressed.** `spaceById(spaces, id)` is the one place a client's string becomes a space: it throws "Invalid space id." for anything that is not a Durable Object id of the spaces namespace, which the id of a workspace or of a user is not. `AuthenticatedApi.openSpace` goes through it directly; `newWorkspace`, `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint` and `Overseer.moveToSpace` go through `UserDurableObject.#teamSpaceId()`, before the workspace's record is written. `#teamSpaceId()` also returns the id in the spelling the space itself reports, and that spelling is what a record holds.

What each entry point does with the id a client gives it:

| The id is | `openSpace(id)` | `newWorkspace(spaceId)`, `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint(…, spaceId)` | `Overseer.moveToSpace(spaceId)`, called by the owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| not a space id | throws "Invalid space id." | throws "Invalid space id."; no workspace is created and nothing is recorded | throws "Invalid space id."; nothing is recorded |
| well formed, but names no space | throws `noSuchSpace()` | the workspace is created and its record points at the id; at its first activity the space refuses it and it falls back to the owner's personal space, with no error (for one made from a blueprint, right after creation) | for a workspace that has seen activity: throws `noSuchSpace()`, and the workspace is not moved. For a provisional one: recorded, and it falls back at its first activity |
| a space the caller is not a member of | throws `noSuchSpace()` | as the row above | as the row above |
| another user's personal space | throws `noSuchSpace()`: the caller is not a member | as the row above: only its owner adds to a personal space | as the row above |
| the caller's own personal space | opens it once it exists; until then throws `noSuchSpace()`, as the id then names no space | the same as passing nothing: the record gets no `spaceId` | the same as `null` |
| a team space the caller may add to | opens it | recorded; the space lists the workspace at its first activity | moved |

`openSpace` asks the space for the caller's membership at once. `newWorkspace`, `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint` and `Overseer.moveToSpace` make no membership check of their own when they take an id: membership is checked when the space is asked to list the workspace (see "Workspaces in a space"), which a move of a workspace that has seen activity does within the call. The three cases of a space that is not the caller's to use are indistinguishable from one another at every entry point. And `WorkspaceMetadata.spaceId` is never the id of the owner's own personal space: it is a team space's id once that space lists the workspace, and until then it says only where the workspace was asked to go. A move by anyone but the workspace's owner is refused before the id is looked at.

A personal space exists from the first time it is needed, not from the creation of the account: `listSpaces()` creates it, and so does the first sync of a workspace of the user's that belongs in it (`UserDurableObject.#ensurePersonalSpace()`). Its id is the same before and after, but until then it is one more id that names no space, and `openSpace`, which creates nothing, answers its own owner as it answers anyone. A client meets this only if it holds the id from somewhere other than `listSpaces()`, the one call that hands it out. The two workspace columns of that row do not depend on it: there the id is compared, never opened.

## Personal and team spaces

There are two kinds of space (`SpaceKind`), fixed when the space is created. Spaces form a flat list: a space has no parent and contains no other space.

| | Personal space | Team space |
|---|---|---|
| Created | For each user, the first time it is needed: by `listSpaces()`, or by a workspace of theirs that belongs in it | By any signed-in user, with `createSpace(name)` |
| Id | Derived from the owner's profile id | Made by the server when the space is created |
| Name | The owner's display name when the space was created | Given by the creator: trimmed, then 1 to 100 characters; it need not be unique |
| Admins | The owner, and nobody else | One or more of its members |
| Other members | None | Allowed, in any role |
| Adds workspaces | The owner, and nobody else | Any member, whatever their role |

**Creating.** `SpaceModel.create({ name, kind }, creator)` creates the space: in one transaction it writes `info` and makes `creator` the first admin and, for a personal space, the owner, so a creation that fails leaves no space. It is idempotent: creating a space that exists changes nothing, so a creation can never take a space over. It returns whether the space is the one created for `creator`: true when this call created it, true when it already exists as the personal space `creator` owns, and false otherwise, so a team space that exists answers false to everyone.

`createSpace(name)` trims and checks the name (`teamSpaceName()`, "A space name is 1 to 100 characters."), makes a fresh id, creates the space under it and opens it, and that open is what records the space in the creator's mirror. It does not read what `create` returns: an id made a moment before names no space, so there is none to find there.

`UserDurableObject.#ensurePersonalSpace()` creates the user's personal space whenever their mirror does not hold it, and then writes the mirror record itself. Creating it again changes nothing, so the call is safe to repeat and concurrent callers need not take turns. `listSpaces()` calls it, and so does whatever may ask the personal space to list a workspace with no `spaceId` (`#reconcileSpace()`, `#backfillSpaces()`). An answer of false would mean that the space under the user's id was created for someone else, which only a corrupted deployment could give, and it throws ("This user's personal space was created for someone else.").

## Members and roles

Every member holds exactly one `SpaceMemberRole`, and being a member in any role is what lets a user open the space:

- **`admin`** -- may also change the member list of a team space (`Space.setMemberRole`, `Space.removeMember`). On the workspaces themselves an admin holds `build`, and no more.
- **`build`** and **`use`** -- ordinary members, named after the `CollaboratorRole` level (see docs/sharing.md) each holds on the workspaces the space lists. In the space itself the two confer the same thing: reading its info, its member list and its listing of workspaces, adding workspaces of their own to a team space, and leaving it.

In a team space, `setMemberRole(username, role)` sets a role *exactly*: it adds a non-member, and raises or lowers an existing member, who keeps their `added` date. `removeMember(profileId)` lets an admin remove anyone and any other member remove only themself, which is how one leaves a space; an admin removing someone who is not a member does nothing. Removing a member, or lowering one to `use`, also ends the sessions they hold through the role they had (see "Leases and revocations").

A refused open does not say whether the space exists: an id that names no space and a space the caller does not belong to are refused with one error (`noSuchSpace()`; see "Ids").

`SpaceModel` holds the rules of a space, for its members and for its listing, as pure logic over typed storage, with no RPC, so they are unit-tested directly (`packages/workshop-backend/__tests__/spaces.test.ts`). `SpaceDurableObject` is a thin shell around it that adds the account lookup, the mirror pushes and the alarm that delivers revocations.

- **A space always has at least one admin.** Demoting or removing its last admin throws.
- **A personal space's owner is its only member.** The owner is stored as its sole admin, written there by `create()` alone, and so, by the rule above, can be neither demoted nor removed. `setMemberRole` refuses in a personal space whoever the target ("A personal space has no members."), so a personal space never gains a member. A workspace in a personal space is shared through its own sharing; teams use team spaces.

## Workspaces in a space

Where a workspace belongs is recorded in two places, and the first is the authority.

- **The pointer** is `spaceId` on the owner's record of the workspace in their User DO (`GadgetRecord`, which extends `WorkspaceMetadata`). It is the id of a team space; absent means the owner's personal space, whose id is never recorded there. Until its space has listed the workspace it says only where the workspace was asked to go (see "Ids"). The Overseer stores nothing about spaces, and a user's record of a workspace shared with them never carries a pointer.
- **The listing** is the space's `workspaces` collection: each workspace's id, title, a snapshot of its owner's profile and when it was created (`SpaceWorkspaceInfo`). `Space.listWorkspaces()` returns it to any member, newest first. It holds only workspaces that have seen activity (below).

The pointer is the authority because the owner's record is already where the kernel keeps everything else a listing shows or depends on -- that the workspace exists, its title, when it was created, whether it has seen activity -- and is what a delete removes. So a space is told where a workspace belongs and is never asked. The listing follows the pointer and can lag it (below), so where a workspace belongs is never decided from the listing. Whether a member may open it takes both: the pointer has to name the space and the space has to list the workspace under that owner (see "Access through membership"), so a listing that lags gives a role to nobody the pointer does not. Beyond that an entry decides only its own upkeep: it is updated or dropped only by the owner it is listed under.

**Who may add.** `SpaceModel.canAddWorkspaces(profileId)`: only the owner of a personal space; any member of a team space, `use` included. `attachWorkspaces(owner, registrations)` is an upsert that returns `false`, having changed nothing, if it refuses any registration. A workspace not yet listed needs `canAddWorkspaces(owner.id)`. One listed under the same owner is updated whether or not they may still add, which is how a workspace keeps its place, and a current title, after its owner leaves the space. One listed under another owner is refused. `detachWorkspace(id, ownerId)` removes an entry only if that owner holds it, and with it revokes the leases on that workspace.

### Registration and the marker

A workspace registers with its space at its first activity, never when its record is created. `AuthenticatedApi.newWorkspace` creates a workspace *provisional*, before the user has sent anything, and it may never be used, so a record with no `lastActive` is listed nowhere. For `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint` the first activity is part of the creation: instantiating the blueprint marks the workspace active, which starts its registration. Both check only that a `spaceId` is a space id (see "Ids") and store the pointer.

`registered` on the record is the marker: the `{ spaceId, title }` a space last acknowledged, where `spaceId` is the id of that space, personal or team. A marker with no `title` names a space that has acknowledged nothing: it was asked to list the workspace and may or may not have. A marker is written before any space is first asked to list the workspace, so a record with no marker is on no listing. It is the User DO's own bookkeeping: `UserDurableObject.listWorkspaces()` and `getWorkspace()` strip it.

`UserDurableObject.#reconcileSpace(id)` is what brings a listing in line with its record; the only other things the owner's User DO writes to a listing with are the catch-up's pages and a delete (both below). Its target is the space the record points at. It does nothing for a provisional workspace, for one shared with this user, or for one listed as it should be: the marker names the target with the record's current title. Otherwise it **attaches** the workspace to the target, having first written a marker without a title if the record had none; then **detaches** it from the space the marker named, if that is another; and only then **writes the marker**.

A failure leaves the marker out of step, so the next reconcile does whatever is left. A refused attach has changed nothing: a marker written just before it is removed again. An attach or a detach that fails without an answer may or may not have been applied by the space; the next reconcile starts again from the attach, which is safe because the attach is an upsert and the detach does nothing for an entry already gone. Until then a workspace on its way from one space to another may be listed in both; because the attach comes first, no failed reconcile takes a workspace off the one listing it was on. Everything that touches a listing runs on one chain per User DO (`#inSpaceOrder()`), so reconciles never interleave, whichever workspaces they are for.

`#syncSpace(id)` is the reconcile plus the fallback (see "Moves"). `setWorkspaceLastActive` (the first activity and every later one; a later one finds nothing to do unless a sync or a move that failed left it out of step, which makes the next activity the usual retry) and `updateTitle` run it detached, logging a failure as `space.workspace.sync.failed`, so that it neither slows nor fails the Overseer's call. The move awaits it, and the catch-up runs it.

### Moves

**A sync falls back.** When a space refuses the attach during `#syncSpace` -- the owner may not add to the space the record names, and the workspace is not already listed there -- the record is pointed back at the team space that still lists the workspace or, if none does, at the owner's personal space, and reconciled again; `space.workspace.fallback` is logged at `info`. The space that still lists the workspace is the one the marker names, and the fallback tells a team marker from the personal one by comparing it with the personal space's id, so a workspace that goes back to the personal space is left with no `spaceId`. So a workspace created in a space its owner may not add to, or under an id that names no space, ends up in their personal space, with no error.

**A move refuses.** `Overseer.moveToSpace(spaceId)` is for the workspace's owner only and calls their `UserDurableObject.setWorkspaceSpace(id, spaceId)`; `null` means the personal space, and so does the id of the owner's own personal space. A string that is no space id throws "Invalid space id." before anything is recorded. Otherwise the call syncs the workspace where it is, then points the record at the target and reconciles. If the target refuses, because the id names no space, a space the owner is not a member of or someone else's personal space, the record is pointed back where it was and the call throws `noSuchSpace()`, the refusal an open gives, so the move has changed nothing and the answer does not say whether the space exists; the sync before it may have changed something all the same, for example by finishing an earlier move or falling back from one. If a call that reconcile makes to a space fails without an answer it may still have reached the space, so the record stays pointed at the target, and the next sync either finishes the move or, on a refusal, falls back to where the workspace is listed. For a provisional workspace a move only changes the pointer, whatever the id names: the space is asked at the workspace's first activity, and a refusal then falls back as above.

### Delete

`Overseer.deleteSelf()` is for the workspace's owner only. Once its running agents are cancelled and its enabled hooks disabled, it commits the deletion: from then on the workspace refuses every open as not found, and one removal is queued for its owner and for each collaborator of its own sharing. A member who reaches the workspace through its space alone has no record of it, so none is queued for them.

Each removal is a call to that user's `UserDurableObject.workspaceRemoved(id)`, which is `deleteWorkspace(id)` and then arms the user's expiry sweep. For the owner, `deleteWorkspace` first leaves every space that may list the workspace: it detaches it from the space the marker names and from the space the record points at; with no marker no space lists it, and none is told. For a record with no `spaceId` the space it points at is the personal space, and leaving its listing takes that space's id, not the space, so a deletion never creates a personal space. It takes the record as it stands. It runs no sync, so it lists the workspace nowhere, whatever state the record was in. Only then are the record, its outputs and its unread state dropped.

If a space cannot be reached, the call fails with the record and its marker unchanged, because once the record is gone nothing would ever remove the entry. The workspace repeats each removal that has not succeeded from its alarm, and wipes its own storage only once every one has. `detachWorkspace` does nothing for an entry already gone, so a repeat is safe.

Dropping the entry revokes the leases on it (see "Leases and revocations"), and those revocations reach the workspace while it is still removing its other readers' records. A workspace that is being deleted takes none of them: a restart at that point would cut the removals short. It restarts when every removal has been tried, which ends every session, a member's included. Until then what it remembers of each member stands, so an observation is still checked against them and kept from whoever it excludes. A restart of another cause inside that time, a collaborator removed at the same moment for instance, cuts the owner's call short, and the workspace finishes the deletion from its alarm. The tests are in `packages/workshop-backend/__tests__/spaces-workspaces.test.ts`, `spaces-restricted.test.ts` and `spaces-roles.test.ts`.

### Catch-up

`listSpaces()` starts `#backfillSpaces()` without waiting for it, once per Durable Object instance and again after one that failed (`space.workspace.backfill.failed`). It brings the listing of every workspace of the user's own that has seen activity in line with its record, in two steps. It goes by the owner's records alone and calls no workspace's Overseer.

1. **Register with the personal space.** The workspaces that have no pointer and are not yet acknowledged as they are now, which includes every one older than the listing, go to the personal space in pages of up to 128 per `attachWorkspaces` call. Each that has no marker gets one without a title before the call, and each page is marked once acknowledged, so a catch-up that stops resumes with what is left. A workspace whose marker names another space is left to the next step, since that space has to drop it.
2. **Sync the rest.** Every other record out of step goes through `#syncSpace`. That makes the catch-up the retry for a sync that failed earlier on a workspace that has seen no activity since.

## Access through membership

A member's role applies to every workspace the space lists, with no share from the workspace's owner: `admin` and `build` open it to build, and `use` opens every gadget of it to use, each verified when it is got. The role is joined with the workspace's own sharing, so a user opens what either gives them. How the two are joined, verified, kept from granting anything to others and revoked is docs/sharing.md, "Space roles"; this section says what the space and the owner's User DO contribute. The tests of both halves are `packages/workshop-backend/__tests__/spaces-roles.test.ts`.

- **Both sides have to agree.** Unless the workspace's own sharing already gives the user `build`, the workspace's Overseer asks its owner's User DO (`UserDurableObject.workspaceRoleInSpace(id, profileId)`), which answers only for a workspace of the user's own that has seen activity, and then asks the team space the record points at. That space gives a role only to a member, and only for a workspace it lists under that owner (`SpaceModel.workspaceRole`). So a provisional workspace, one whose pointer names a space that does not list it yet, and one listed by a space the pointer has moved away from all give no role. A workspace with no `spaceId` is in its owner's personal space, which has no members besides the owner, so it gives nobody a space role and no space is asked.
- **The owner is not affected.** A workspace keeps its individual owner, in a team space too and after that person leaves the space. Only the owner moves or deletes it, and membership gives nobody the power to share it.
- **A role given is a lease.** Each answer that gives a role is remembered, so that removing or lowering the member, or the workspace leaving the listing, reaches the sessions already open (see "Leases and revocations").
- **Reached through the space.** A workspace a member opens through their role alone is not added to their own list of workspaces (`listWorkspaces()`), and the member is not among its collaborators: the space's listing is where they find it. Someone who is also a collaborator has a record of it, which carries what the workspace's own sharing gives them.
- **The two flags change nothing here.** A workspace that holds restricted data (docs/observers.md) or is owner-invites-only (docs/sharing.md, "`ownerInvitesOnly`") is listed by its space and opened by its members like any other, and stays so when a flag is set: only its Overseer holds the flags, and neither the owner's record nor the space is told them. That is sound because the listing and the role decide who may ask to open the workspace, not who may see what it read: every member is verified as an observer exactly as a collaborator who holds the same access, at `open()` to build and at each `getGadget()` to use, and is refused when that fails; and restricted mode (the hosts a web fetch may reach, manual approval of every action) applies to whoever is in the workspace. Under `ownerInvitesOnly` the sharing graph's rule holds in full: only the owner's direct grants count in the graph, share links admit nobody, and only the owner adds collaborators. The space role is joined with what the graph gives, as on any workspace: the owner chose the space, by creating the workspace in it or moving it there, and the space's admins choose its members.

## Leases and revocations

The space answers one question about its workspaces for the rest of the kernel: which role a profile holds on one, as a member (`SpaceModel.workspaceRole(id, ownerId, profileId)`; see "Access through membership"). What it has answered, it has to be able to take back, so it keeps two more collections.

- `leases` -- one `SpaceLease`, a (workspace, profile) pair, for every answer that gave a role and has not been taken back since: the sessions a change to the members or to the listing may have to end. Every answer that gives a role writes one, whatever the workspace asked for: an open, each `getGadget()` of a `use` session, a check of who an observation must be kept from. Asking again rewrites the same lease, and an answer of no role leaves none. Keyed `<workspace>:<profile>`, so one workspace's leases are a key prefix, and indexed by profile.
- `revocations` -- the leases taken back and not yet delivered, each a `SpaceRevocation`: the lease, a `seq` from `nextRevocation` that is never reused, `due`, the time of its next attempt, and `retryMs`, how long it last waited for one. A new revocation has both at 0, which makes it due at once. The index `byDue` is the order they are attempted in: by when each is due and, among those due together, as queued.

**What takes a lease back.** `removeMember` revokes every lease of that member; `setMemberRole`, those of a member lowered from a role that gave `build` (`admin` or `build`) to `use`; `detachWorkspace`, every lease on that workspace, whatever took it off the listing: a move or a deletion. A member raised, or moved between `admin` and `build`, loses nothing. The lease is deleted and its revocation queued in the same turn as the change, and the alarm is set for now in that turn too, so the three are stored together. A role given again before the revocation is delivered is a new lease; the revocation still goes out.

**Delivery.** `SpaceDurableObject.alarm()` takes up to 16 of the queued revocations that are due, the longest due first, and for each calls `OverseerDurableObject.revokeSpaceAccess(profile)` on the workspace's Overseer. On arrival the workspace restarts if it remembers giving that profile a role, which it then forgets, and changes nothing otherwise, nor while it is being deleted (see "Delete"; docs/sharing.md, "Space roles"). A revocation leaves the queue only once its call has returned. One whose call fails is logged (`space.revocation.deliver.failed`) and kept, due again after a wait of its own that doubles with each failure from one second to five minutes. That wait is stored with the revocation, so it does not start over when the space's object is discarded from memory, and it holds up no other: a revocation queued later is due at once and attempted in the next run, however many are waiting to be retried. While any remain the alarm is set for when the next is due, which is now while any is. No Overseer is ever polled: a workspace hears from its space only when a role the space gave out on it has ended.

## Where the state lives

**One Durable Object per space.** Each space is a `SpaceDurableObject`, addressed by the space's id. Its storage (`storage-schema/space-storage.ts`) is a singleton `info` -- the name, kind and, for a personal space, the owner, but no id, which is the object's own; absent until the space is created and never removed -- a collection `members` keyed by profile id, a collection `workspaces` keyed by workspace id (the listing, see "Workspaces in a space"), and two collections behind the roles its members hold on those workspaces, `leases` and `revocations`, with the counter `nextRevocation` (see "Leases and revocations"). A personal space's owner is stored in `members` too, as its only admin and only member.

**Nothing lists the spaces.** No object lists the spaces of a deployment. A space is reached by its id, which a member reads from their own list of spaces, and whether an id names a space is asked of the object under it.

**The user's own Durable Object** holds (`storage-schema/user-storage.ts`) its records of the user's workspaces, each with its `spaceId` and its marker (see "Workspaces in a space"), and `spaces`, a **mirror** of the user's memberships: one `SpaceInfo` per space, keyed by space id, with `role` set to that user's role. The mirror authorizes nothing. Nothing is stored to find the personal space, whose id is derived from the user's profile id. Once the mirror holds the personal space, `listSpaces()` is answered from the user's own object alone, waiting on no call to any space.

### The mirror

The mirror is presentation only, like the record a recipient's account keeps of a workspace shared with them (`UserDurableObject.recordSharedWorkspaceOpen()`, see docs/sharing.md). Every operation on a space is decided from what the space itself stores at the time of the call -- its `info`, its member list and, for an entry of its listing, the owner that entry is listed under (see "Workspaces in a space"); nothing is ever authorized from the mirror.

The space keeps it current by pushing to the affected user's object -- `recordSpaceMembership(info)` after `setMemberRole`, `forgetSpace(id)` after `removeMember`. The push is best-effort: the member list is already written, so a push that fails is logged (`space.membership.mirror.failed`) and the change stands. Opening is what heals a lost push: before `SpaceDurableObject.open` answers, the space makes the same push for the caller, recording their membership if they have one and dropping the space from their mirror if they do not. So a listed space may still refuse to open, and a membership the mirror missed appears once that space has been opened by id. A space that was never created pushes nothing: it has never had a member, so no mirror holds it.

### Trust boundary

Every method `SpaceDurableObject` exposes takes the acting user as a plain parameter -- their profile id, or for `create` and `attachWorkspaces` their profile -- like `OverseerDurableObject.open()`. Its only callers are `AuthenticatedApiImpl` (server.ts), which passes the identity of the authenticated session, and `UserDurableObject` (user.ts), which passes its own -- never a client, gadget, gatekeeper or agent -- so the parameter is authoritative.

`attachWorkspaces`, `detachWorkspace` and `workspaceRole` are called by `UserDurableObject` alone, which states its own user as the owner, so a user's workspaces are only ever registered or dropped by that user's object, and a space is only ever asked for a role on a workspace by the object whose record places it there. None is on `Space`. `UserDurableObject.workspaceRoleInSpace` in turn is called only by a workspace's Overseer, about itself, and `OverseerDurableObject.revokeSpaceAccess` only by a space's alarm; all it can do is end sessions, which then reopen and are authorized afresh.

A client only ever holds a `Space`: the `SpaceClientInterface` minted by `SpaceDurableObject.open()`, which closes over the caller fixed at open time. It carries no standing permission. Every method has the space look that user's membership up again, so a stub held by someone who has since been removed or demoted loses those powers at once, with no session to terminate. A workspace they have open is another matter: that session was authorized when it opened, and is what a revocation ends.

`setMemberRole` checks that the caller is an admin *before* it resolves the username, so a non-admin cannot use it to find out which accounts exist. The lookup is the one `Overseer.addCollaborator` uses (`UserDurableObject.whoamiIfExists()`), and likewise returns null for a username with no account.

## API surface

- `AuthenticatedApi.listSpaces()` -- the caller's spaces from their mirror: their personal space first, then the others by name. It creates the caller's personal space if it does not exist yet.
- `AuthenticatedApi.openSpace(id)` -- a `Space` acting as the caller. A string that is no space id throws; an id the caller cannot open gets the single refusal (see "Ids").
- `AuthenticatedApi.createSpace(name)` -- creates a team space under an id the server makes and opens it, with the caller as its first admin.
- `Space` -- `getInfo()`, `listMembers()` and `listWorkspaces()` for any member; `setMemberRole(username, role)` for admins of a team space; `removeMember(profileId)` for an admin, or a member removing themself. The last two also change who may open the workspaces the space lists.
- `AuthenticatedApi.openWorkspace(id)` -- opens, besides a workspace the caller owns or one shared with them, one that belongs to a space they are a member of and that the space lists (see "Access through membership"). `WorkspaceMetadata.role` on the session is what the membership and the workspace's own sharing give, joined, and does not say which it came from.
- `AuthenticatedApi.newWorkspace(spaceId?)` and `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint(blueprintId, bindings, spaceId?)` -- create the workspace in a team space; omitted, or given the id of the caller's own personal space, in the caller's personal space. A string that is no space id throws, and no workspace is created.
- `Overseer.moveToSpace(spaceId | null)` -- owner only: moves the workspace to a team space, or with `null`, or the id of the owner's own personal space, back to its owner's personal space.
- `WorkspaceMetadata.spaceId` -- the id of the team space a workspace belongs to, on its owner's own record only (so in `listWorkspaces()` for a workspace the caller owns); absent means the owner's personal space.

## Known limitations

- **A space cannot be deleted or renamed.** Nothing deletes a space, nothing changes a team space's name, and a personal space's name is the snapshot taken when it was created.
- **Unread state and notifications skip members.** The unread counts and notification fan-out of a workspace go to its owner and to the `build` collaborators of its own sharing (`deliverDirtyChats`, `mayReadChatState` in `overseer.ts`). Someone who reaches it only through a space gets neither, and it is not in their own list of workspaces.
- **Names are not unique.** Two team spaces may carry the same name, and only the id tells them apart. A `createSpace` that is repeated after its answer was lost makes a second space.
- **Display names are snapshots.** The display name in a member record, in `SpaceInfo.owner`, in a personal space's `name` and in a listed workspace's `owner` may trail a rename. A listed workspace's title trails a change until its next sync.
- **Ending a role restarts the whole workspace.** A revocation disconnects every session of the workspace, the owner's included, not only the affected member's. It does so whenever the workspace's Overseer, since it last started, gave that member a role, whether or not anything of theirs is still open: it may have learned the role while checking an observation that excludes them, and it cannot tell that a client kept nothing a closed session handed out. Each client reopens and is authorized afresh.
- **A session outlives its role for a moment.** Between the change in the space and the alarm delivering the revocation, a session already open continues, and longer while the workspace's Overseer cannot be reached: the revocation is retried, with waits of up to five minutes, for as long as it takes. A workspace on its way to another space keeps the sessions opened through the space it is leaving until that space drops its entry, which a failed detach puts off to the next sync. Opens are not subject to this window, since each asks the space as it stands.
- **Leases are only ever revoked.** A lease stays until its member is removed or lowered or its workspace leaves the listing, whether or not the session it was given for is still open, so a workspace leaving a listing queues one revocation for every lease still standing on it: one for each member answered with a role on it since their leases were last revoked. (A member lowered to `use` had theirs revoked then, and holds one again only if answered with a role afterwards.) Its Overseer ignores each one for a member it gave no role since it last started and is not asking about, and every one while it is being deleted (see "Delete").
- **A late revocation restarts a workspace whose member still has a role.** A revocation that arrives after its member regained a role by another route, for example on a workspace moved between two spaces they belong to and reopened before the space it left was heard from, finds that role remembered and restarts the workspace once; the member reopens with the access they had.
- **A member can be turned away once while roles are being revoked.** At an open, a lookup that revocations of other members' roles overtake twice in a row counts as no space role (docs/sharing.md, "Lookups in flight"), so in that rare interleaving a member opens with what the workspace's own sharing gives them, or is refused if it gives nothing, and opens as a member on trying again.
- **A workspace does not show who reaches it through its space.** Its collaborator list holds only the people its own sharing admits, and a session does not say whether its role came from the space, so a member who builds through the space and tries to share finds out from the refusal. The Share modal of an owner-invites-only workspace says that only the owner can add people, which is true of its collaborators: the admins of its space still admit members, who open it in their role.
- **A listing shows a workspace to members before they can open it.** Every member of a space sees the title, owner and creation date of each workspace it lists, including a member who has not passed, or cannot pass, the verification that opening it takes (docs/observers.md), a workspace that holds restricted data included. The title shown may be one a model generated from the workspace's chat.
- **A workspace cannot be evicted.** It stays in a team space after its owner leaves or is removed, and only its owner can move or delete it: a space admin cannot take it off the listing. The space's members keep their roles on it meanwhile, and its owner, no longer a member, still owns it.
- **Membership is checked at registration, not at creation.** `newWorkspace(spaceId)` and `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint(…, spaceId)` accept any space id, and a workspace whose owner may not add to that space falls back to their personal space at its first activity (for one made from a blueprint, as it is created), with nothing reported to the caller.
- **A workspace can be listed by two spaces.** During a move both list it between the attach and the detach, and when either call fails, possibly until the next sync. Until then both show it, though only the members of the space its record points at can open it through their membership.
- **A deleted workspace can stay listed for a moment.** While a space cannot be reached, its entry and the owner's record stand until the workspace's repeated removal gets through (see "Delete"); opening the workspace is refused as not found meanwhile.
- **A collaborator's Outputs index can outlast a space role.** For someone who is both a collaborator and a member, the index is written from everything they can open at the time, at each open and while their session lasts. Gadgets reached only through the space stay in it until their next open after the role ends, and opening one is refused.
- **The listing is not paginated.** `listWorkspaces()` returns every workspace of the space in one answer.
- **A lost mirror write hides a space until it is opened.** A member whose mirror write was lost finds the space again by opening it by id. A team space whose creator received no answer to `createSpace`, and whose mirror write was lost as well, cannot be found: nothing lists the spaces, and nobody holds its id.
