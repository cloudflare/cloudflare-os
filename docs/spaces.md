# Spaces

A space groups users and workspaces: it is an id, a display name, a list of members and a listing of the workspaces that belong to it. Every user has one **personal space**, and any signed-in user may create **team spaces**. A space may hold **sub-spaces**, whose members include those of the spaces they are inside (see "Sub-spaces"). Every workspace belongs to exactly one space: its owner's personal space, unless the owner placed it in a team space. The member lists of a space and of the spaces it is inside are the only authority on who belongs to it and in what role; the owner's record of a workspace is the only authority on which space it belongs to. The kernel side is `packages/workshop-backend/src/spaces.ts`, the "Spaces" and "Workspaces in spaces" parts of `user.ts` and the "Space roles" part of `overseer.ts`; the API is listed under "API surface" below. The Workshop's UI for spaces is behind the `spaces` UI flag.

Belonging to a space decides where a workspace is listed, who sees it listed and who may open it as a member: a member's role applies to every workspace the space lists (see "Access through membership"). The workspace's own sharing applies alongside, and a user opens with what either gives them (see docs/sharing.md, "Space roles"). A workspace that holds restricted data or is owner-invites-only is listed and opened like any other (see "Access through membership").

## Ids

A space is addressed by its id and by nothing else. The id is the id of the space's Durable Object (`SpaceDurableObject`), a string of 64 hex digits that clients treat as opaque (`SpaceInfo.id`). It is unique and immutable. The Workshop's page for a space is `/spaces/<id>`; a workspace's stays `/workspace/<id>`.

- **Where an id comes from.** A team space's id is made by the server when the space is created (`SpaceDurableObject.newUniqueId()` in `AuthenticatedApiImpl.createSpace` and, for a sub-space, in `SpaceDurableObject.createSubspace`); a caller never supplies it. A personal space's is derived from its owner (`SpaceDurableObject.idFromName(profile.id)`), so it is the same on every call and nothing is stored to find it. The object takes its id from `ctx.id`: no caller tells a space its id, and the space does not store it.
- **An id is not a capability.** Every call has the space look the acting user's membership up at that moment, so knowing an id opens nothing. An id that names no space, a space the caller is not a member of, and another user's personal space are all refused with one error, `noSuchSpace()` ("No such space, or you are not a member of it."), so the answer does not say whether the space exists.
- **A string that is not an id is refused before it is recorded or addressed.** `spaceById(spaces, id)` is the one place a client's string becomes a space: it throws "Invalid space id." for anything that is not a Durable Object id of the spaces namespace, which the id of a workspace or of a user is not. `AuthenticatedApi.openSpace` goes through it directly; `newWorkspace`, `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint` and `Overseer.moveToSpace` go through `UserDurableObject.#teamSpaceId()`, before the workspace's record is written. `#teamSpaceId()` also returns the id in the spelling the space itself reports, and that spelling is what a record holds.

What each entry point does with the id a client gives it:

| The id is | `openSpace(id)` | `newWorkspace(spaceId)`, `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint(…, spaceId)` | `Overseer.moveToSpace(spaceId)`, called by the owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| not a space id | throws "Invalid space id." | throws "Invalid space id."; no workspace is created and nothing is recorded | throws "Invalid space id."; nothing is recorded |
| well formed, but names no space | throws `noSuchSpace()` | the workspace is created and its record points at the id; at its first activity the space refuses it and it falls back to the owner's personal space, with no error (for one made from a blueprint, right after creation) | for a workspace that has seen activity: throws `noSuchSpace()`, and the workspace is not moved. For a provisional one: recorded, and it falls back at its first activity |
| a space the caller is not a member of | throws `noSuchSpace()` | as the row above | as the row above |
| another user's personal space | throws `noSuchSpace()`: the caller is not a member | as the row above: only its owner adds to a personal space | as the row above |
| the caller's own personal space | opens it once it exists; until then throws `noSuchSpace()`, as the id then names no space | the same as passing nothing: the record gets no `spaceId` | the same as `null` |
| a team space the caller may add to | opens it | recorded; the space lists the workspace at its first activity | moved |

In every row a member of a space is whoever holds a role in it, given there or inherited from a space it is inside (see "Sub-spaces"). A sub-space is a team space in every row, and the parent of a sub-space someone was added to refuses them like any space they hold no role in.

`openSpace` asks the space for the caller's membership at once. `newWorkspace`, `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint` and `Overseer.moveToSpace` make no membership check of their own when they take an id: membership is checked when the space is asked to list the workspace (see "Workspaces in a space"), which a move of a workspace that has seen activity does within the call. The three cases of a space that is not the caller's to use are indistinguishable from one another at every entry point. And `WorkspaceMetadata.spaceId` is never the id of the owner's own personal space: it is a team space's id once that space lists the workspace, and until then it says only where the workspace was asked to go. A move by anyone but the workspace's owner is refused before the id is looked at.

A personal space exists from the first time it is needed, not from the creation of the account: `listSpaces()` creates it, and so does the first sync of a workspace of the user's that belongs in it (`UserDurableObject.#ensurePersonalSpace()`). Its id is the same before and after, but until then it is one more id that names no space, and `openSpace`, which creates nothing, answers its own owner as it answers anyone. A client meets this only if it holds the id from somewhere other than the two calls that hand it out: `listSpaces()`, which creates the space, and `getInfo()` on a sub-space created in it, which tells its owner the id as `SpaceInfo.parent` (see "Sub-spaces"). Such a sub-space exists only once the personal space does, since a sub-space is created by the space it is in. The two workspace columns of that row do not depend on it: there the id is compared, never opened.

## Personal and team spaces

There are two kinds of space (`SpaceKind`), fixed when the space is created. A space of either kind may hold sub-spaces, which are always team spaces, down to `MAX_SPACE_DEPTH` (5) levels below a **root**, a space that is inside no other (see "Sub-spaces"). The table describes the two kinds of root; a sub-space is a team space that differs from a team root in how it is created, in starting with no member of its own and in needing no admin of its own.

| | Personal space | Team space |
|---|---|---|
| Created | For each user, the first time it is needed: by `listSpaces()`, or by a workspace of theirs that belongs in it | By any signed-in user, with `createSpace(name)` |
| Id | Derived from the owner's profile id | Made by the server when the space is created |
| Name | The owner's display name when the space was created | Given by the creator: trimmed, then 1 to 100 characters; it need not be unique |
| Admins | The owner, and nobody else | One or more of its members |
| Other members | None | Allowed, in any role |
| Adds workspaces | The owner, and nobody else | Any member, whatever their role |

**Creating.** `SpaceModel.create({ name, kind }, creator)` creates the space: in one transaction it writes `info` and makes `creator` the first admin and, for a personal space, the owner, so a creation that fails leaves no space. It is idempotent: creating a space that exists changes nothing, so a creation can never take a space over. It returns whether the space is the one created for `creator`: true when this call created it, true when it already exists as the personal space `creator` owns, and false otherwise, so a team space that exists answers false to everyone. A sub-space is created with no creator, by the space it is made in (see "Sub-spaces", "Creating a sub-space").

`createSpace(name)` trims and checks the name (`teamSpaceName()`, "A space name is 1 to 100 characters."), makes a fresh id, creates the space under it and opens it, and that open is what records the space in the creator's mirror. It does not read what `create` returns: an id made a moment before names no space, so there is none to find there.

`UserDurableObject.#ensurePersonalSpace()` creates the user's personal space whenever their mirror does not hold it, and then writes the mirror record itself. Creating it again changes nothing, so the call is safe to repeat and concurrent callers need not take turns. `listSpaces()` calls it, and so does whatever may ask the personal space to list a workspace with no `spaceId` (`#reconcileSpace()`, `#backfillSpaces()`). An answer of false would mean that the space under the user's id was created for someone else, which only a corrupted deployment could give, and it throws ("This user's personal space was created for someone else.").

## Members and roles

Every member holds exactly one `SpaceMemberRole` in a space: in a root the role they were given there, in a sub-space the higher of that and the role they inherit from the spaces it is inside (see "Sub-spaces"). Holding a role, whichever it is, is what lets a user open the space:

- **`admin`** -- may also change the member list of a team space (`Space.setMemberRole`, `Space.removeMember`). On the workspaces themselves an admin holds `build`, and no more.
- **`build`** and **`use`** -- ordinary members, named after the `CollaboratorRole` level (see docs/sharing.md) each holds on the workspaces the space lists. In the space itself both confer reading its info, its member list and its listings of workspaces and of sub-spaces, adding workspaces of their own to a team space, and leaving it; `build`, like `admin`, also confers creating a sub-space in it (`Space.createSubspace`).

`setMemberRole` and `removeMember` act on the members the space has of its own: the people given a role in that space, as distinct from those who hold one there by inheritance. In a team space, `setMemberRole(username, role)` sets the role given there *exactly*: it adds someone who was given none, and raises or lowers one who was, who keeps their `added` date. `removeMember(profileId)` lets an admin remove anyone and any other member remove only themself, which is how one leaves a space; removing someone who was given no role there does nothing. Neither lowers or ends a role inherited from a space above, which ends only where it was given. Removing a member, or lowering one to `use`, also ends the sessions they hold through the role they had (see "Leases and revocations").

A refused open does not say whether the space exists: an id that names no space and a space the caller does not belong to are refused with one error (`noSuchSpace()`; see "Ids").

`SpaceModel` holds the rules of a space, for its members and for its listing, as pure logic over typed storage, with no RPC, so they are unit-tested directly (`packages/workshop-backend/__tests__/spaces.test.ts`). `SpaceDurableObject` is a thin shell around it that adds the account lookup, the mirror pushes, the question a sub-space puts to its ancestors and the alarm that delivers revocations.

- **A root always has at least one admin.** Demoting or removing its last admin throws. A sub-space needs no admin of its own, since it always has the admins it inherits (see "Sub-spaces", "Roles are inherited").
- **A personal space's owner is its only member.** The owner is stored as its sole admin, written there by `create()` alone, and so, by the rule above, can be neither demoted nor removed. `setMemberRole` refuses in a personal space whoever the target ("A personal space has no members."), so a personal space never gains a member. A workspace in a personal space is shared through its own sharing; teams use team spaces, which a sub-space of a personal space is too.

## Workspaces in a space

Where a workspace belongs is recorded in two places, and the first is the authority.

- **The pointer** is `spaceId` on the owner's record of the workspace in their User DO (`GadgetRecord`, which extends `WorkspaceMetadata`). It is the id of a team space; absent means the owner's personal space, whose id is never recorded there. Until its space has listed the workspace it says only where the workspace was asked to go (see "Ids"). The Overseer stores nothing about spaces, and a user's record of a workspace shared with them never carries a pointer.
- **The listing** is the space's `workspaces` collection: each workspace's id, title, a snapshot of its owner's profile and when it was created (`SpaceWorkspaceInfo`). `Space.listWorkspaces()` returns it to any member, newest first. It holds only workspaces that have seen activity (below).

The pointer is the authority because the owner's record is already where the kernel keeps everything else a listing shows or depends on -- that the workspace exists, its title, when it was created, whether it has seen activity -- and is what a delete removes. So a space is told where a workspace belongs and is never asked. The listing follows the pointer and can lag it (below), so where a workspace belongs is never decided from the listing. Whether a member may open it takes both: the pointer has to name the space and the space has to list the workspace under that owner (see "Access through membership"), so a listing that lags gives a role to nobody the pointer does not. Beyond that an entry decides only its own upkeep: it is updated or dropped only by the owner it is listed under.

**Who may add.** `SpaceModel.canAddWorkspaces(profileId)`: only the owner of a personal space; any member of a team space, `use` included, whether their role was given there or is inherited. `attachWorkspaces(owner, registrations)` is an upsert that returns `false`, having changed nothing, if it refuses any registration. A workspace not yet listed needs `canAddWorkspaces(owner.id)`. One listed under the same owner is updated whether or not they may still add, which is how a workspace keeps its place, and a current title, after its owner leaves the space. One listed under another owner is refused. `detachWorkspace(id, ownerId)` removes an entry only if that owner holds it, and with it revokes the leases on that workspace.

### Registration and the marker

A workspace registers with its space at its first activity, never when its record is created. `AuthenticatedApi.newWorkspace` creates a workspace *provisional*, before the user has sent anything, and it may never be used, so a record with no `lastActive` is listed nowhere. For `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint` the first activity is part of the creation: instantiating the blueprint marks the workspace active, which starts its registration. Both check only that a `spaceId` is a space id (see "Ids") and store the pointer.

`registered` on the record is the marker: the `{ spaceId, title }` a space last acknowledged, where `spaceId` is the id of that space, personal or team. A marker with no `title` names a space that has acknowledged nothing: it was asked to list the workspace and may or may not have. A marker is written before any space is first asked to list the workspace, so a record with no marker is on no listing. It is the User DO's own bookkeeping: `UserDurableObject.listWorkspaces()` and `getWorkspace()` strip it.

`UserDurableObject.#reconcileSpace(id)` is what brings a listing in line with its record; the only other things the owner's User DO writes to a listing with are the catch-up's pages and a delete (both below). Its target is the space the record points at. It does nothing for a provisional workspace, for one shared with this user, or for one listed as it should be: the marker names the target with the record's current title. Otherwise it **attaches** the workspace to the target, having first written a marker without a title if the record had none; then **detaches** it from the space the marker named, if that is another; and only then **writes the marker**.

A failure leaves the marker out of step, so the next reconcile does whatever is left. A refused attach has changed nothing: a marker written just before it is removed again. An attach or a detach that fails without an answer may or may not have been applied by the space; the next reconcile starts again from the attach, which is safe because the attach is an upsert and the detach does nothing for an entry already gone. Until then a workspace on its way from one space to another may be listed in both; because the attach comes first, no failed reconcile takes a workspace off the one listing it was on. Everything that touches a listing runs on one chain per User DO (`#inSpaceOrder()`), so reconciles never interleave, whichever workspaces they are for.

`#syncSpace(id)` is the reconcile plus the fallback (see "Moves"). `setWorkspaceLastActive` (the first activity and every later one; a later one finds nothing to do unless a sync or a move that failed left it out of step, which makes the next activity the usual retry) and `updateTitle` run it detached, logging a failure as `space.workspace.sync.failed`, so that it neither slows nor fails the Overseer's call. The move awaits it, and the catch-up runs it.

### Moves

**A sync falls back.** When a space refuses the attach during `#syncSpace` -- the owner may not add to the space the record names, and the workspace is not already listed there -- the record is pointed back at the team space that still lists the workspace or, if none does, at the owner's personal space, and reconciled again; `space.workspace.fallback` is logged at `info`. The space that still lists the workspace is the one the marker names, and the fallback tells a team marker from the personal one by comparing it with the personal space's id, so a workspace that goes back to the personal space is left with no `spaceId`. So a workspace created in a space its owner may not add to, or under an id that names no space, ends up in their personal space, with no error.

**A move refuses.** `Overseer.moveToSpace(spaceId)` is for the workspace's owner only and calls their `UserDurableObject.setWorkspaceSpace(id, spaceId)`; `null` means the personal space, and so does the id of the owner's own personal space. A string that is no space id throws "Invalid space id." before anything is recorded. Otherwise the call syncs the workspace where it is, then points the record at the target and reconciles. If the target refuses, because the id names no space, a space the owner is not a member of or someone else's personal space, the record is pointed back where it was and the call throws `noSuchSpace()`, the refusal an open gives, so the move has changed nothing and the answer does not say whether the space exists; the sync before it may have changed something all the same, for example by finishing an earlier move or falling back from one. If a call that reconcile makes to a space fails without an answer it may still have reached the space, so the record stays pointed at the target, and the next sync either finishes the move or, on a refusal, falls back to where the workspace is listed. For a provisional workspace a move only changes the pointer, whatever the id names: the space is asked at the workspace's first activity, and a refusal then falls back as above.

### Delete

`Overseer.deleteSelf()` is for the workspace's owner only. Once its running agents are cancelled and its enabled hooks disabled, it commits the deletion: from then on the workspace refuses every open as not found, and one removal is queued for its owner and for each collaborator of its own sharing. A member who reaches the workspace through its space alone has no record of it, so none is queued for them.

Each removal is a call to that user's `UserDurableObject.workspaceRemoved(id)`, which is `deleteWorkspace(id)` and then arms the user's expiry sweep. For the owner, `deleteWorkspace` first leaves every space that may list the workspace: it detaches it from the space the marker names and from the space the record points at; with no marker no space lists it, and none is told. For a record with no `spaceId` the space it points at is the personal space, and leaving its listing takes that space's id, not the space, so a deletion never creates a personal space. It takes the record as it stands. It runs no sync, so it lists the workspace nowhere, whatever state the record was in. Only then are the record, its outputs and its unread state dropped.

If a space cannot be reached, the call fails with the record and its marker unchanged, because once the record is gone nothing would ever remove the entry. The workspace repeats each removal that has not succeeded from its alarm, and wipes its own storage only once every one has. `detachWorkspace` does nothing for an entry already gone, so a repeat is safe.

Dropping the entry revokes the leases on it (see "Leases and revocations"), and those revocations reach the workspace while it is still removing its other readers' records. A workspace that is being deleted takes none of them: a restart at that point would cut the removals short. It restarts when every removal has been tried, which ends every session, a member's included. Until then what it remembers of each member stands, so an observation is still checked against them and kept from whoever it excludes. A restart of another cause inside that time, a collaborator removed at the same moment for instance, cuts the owner's call short, and the workspace finishes the deletion from its alarm. The tests are in `packages/workshop-backend/__tests__/spaces-workspaces.test.ts`, `spaces-restricted.test.ts` and `spaces-roles.test.ts`.

### Catch-up

`listSpaces()` starts `#backfillSpaces()` without waiting for it, once per Durable Object instance and again after one that failed (`space.workspace.backfill.failed`). It brings the listing of every workspace of the user's own that has seen activity in line with its record, in two steps. It goes by the owner's records alone and calls no workspace's Overseer.

1. **Register with the personal space.** The workspaces that have no pointer and are not yet acknowledged as they are now, which includes every one older than the listing, go to the personal space in pages of up to 128 per `attachWorkspaces` call. Each that has no marker gets one without a title before the call, and each page is marked once acknowledged, so a catch-up that stops resumes with what is left. A workspace whose marker names another space is left to the next step, since that space has to drop it.
2. **Sync the rest.** Every other record out of step goes through `#syncSpace`. That makes the catch-up the retry for a sync that failed earlier on a workspace that has seen no activity since.

## Access through membership

A member's role applies to every workspace the space lists, with no share from the workspace's owner: `admin` and `build` open it to build, and `use` opens every gadget of it to use, each verified when it is got. The role is joined with the workspace's own sharing, so a user opens what either gives them. How the two are joined, verified, kept from granting anything to others and revoked is docs/sharing.md, "Space roles"; this section says what the space and the owner's User DO contribute. The tests of both halves are `packages/workshop-backend/__tests__/spaces-roles.test.ts`.

- **Both sides have to agree.** Unless the workspace's own sharing already gives the user `build`, the workspace's Overseer asks its owner's User DO (`UserDurableObject.workspaceRoleInSpace(id, profileId)`), which answers only for a workspace of the user's own that has seen activity, and then asks the team space the record points at. That space gives a role only to whoever holds one in it, their own or inherited (see "Sub-spaces"), and only for a workspace it lists under that owner (`SpaceModel.workspaceRole`). So a provisional workspace, one whose pointer names a space that does not list it yet, and one listed by a space the pointer has moved away from all give no role. A workspace with no `spaceId` is in its owner's personal space, which has no members besides the owner, so it gives nobody a space role and no space is asked. A workspace in a sub-space of a personal space carries that sub-space's id, and the sub-space is asked like any team space.
- **The owner is not affected.** A workspace keeps its individual owner, in a team space too and after that person leaves the space. Only the owner moves or deletes it, and membership gives nobody the power to share it.
- **A role given is a lease.** Each answer that gives a role is remembered, so that removing or lowering the member, or the workspace leaving the listing, reaches the sessions already open (see "Leases and revocations").
- **Reached through the space.** A workspace a member opens through their role alone is not added to their own list of workspaces (`listWorkspaces()`), and the member is not among its collaborators: the space's listing is where they find it. Someone who is also a collaborator has a record of it, which carries what the workspace's own sharing gives them.
- **The two flags change nothing here.** A workspace that holds restricted data (docs/observers.md) or is owner-invites-only (docs/sharing.md, "`ownerInvitesOnly`") is listed by its space and opened by its members like any other, and stays so when a flag is set: only its Overseer holds the flags, and neither the owner's record nor the space is told them. That is sound because the listing and the role decide who may ask to open the workspace, not who may see what it read: every member is verified as an observer exactly as a collaborator who holds the same access, at `open()` to build and at each `getGadget()` to use, and is refused when that fails; and restricted mode (the hosts a web fetch may reach, manual approval of every action) applies to whoever is in the workspace. Under `ownerInvitesOnly` the sharing graph's rule holds in full: only the owner's direct grants count in the graph, share links admit nobody, and only the owner adds collaborators. The space role is joined with what the graph gives, as on any workspace: the owner chose the space, by creating the workspace in it or moving it there, and the space's admins choose its members.

## Leases and revocations

The space answers one question about its workspaces for the rest of the kernel: which role a profile holds on one, as a member (`SpaceModel.workspaceRole(id, ownerId, profileId)`; see "Access through membership"). It answers one more for the spaces below it: which role a profile was given in it (`SpaceModel.lend(descendant, profileId)`; see "Sub-spaces"). What it has answered, it has to be able to take back, so it keeps two more collections.

- `leases` -- one `SpaceLease` for every answer that gave a role and has not been taken back since, of two kinds. A (workspace, profile) pair stands for the sessions a change to the members or to the listing may have to end. Every answer that gives a role on a workspace writes one, whatever the workspace asked for: an open, each `getGadget()` of a `use` session, a check of who an observation must be kept from. It is keyed `<workspace>:<profile>`, so one workspace's leases are a key prefix. A (space, profile) pair stands for what a space below this one may have given the profile on the strength of the role it was told they hold here; it is keyed `space:<space>:<profile>`, which no workspace's id starts with. For either kind, asking again rewrites the same lease, and an answer of no role leaves none. Both are indexed by profile.
- `revocations` -- the leases taken back and not yet delivered, each a `SpaceRevocation`: the lease, a `seq` from `nextRevocation` that is never reused, `due`, the time of its next attempt, and `retryMs`, how long it last waited for one. A new revocation has both at 0, which makes it due at once. The index `byDue` is the order they are attempted in: by when each is due and, among those due together, as queued.

**What takes a lease back.** `removeMember` revokes every lease of that member, of both kinds, and only when it removed a member of the space itself: removing someone who holds a role there by inheritance alone changes nothing and revokes nothing. `setMemberRole` revokes those of a member lowered from a role that gave `build` (`admin` or `build`) to `use`, of both kinds; `detachWorkspace`, every lease on that workspace, whatever took it off the listing: a move or a deletion; and `revokeInherited(profileId)`, by which an ancestor tells a sub-space that a role it lent has ended, the workspace leases of that profile and none of the other kind (see "Sub-spaces", "Leases and revocation between spaces"). A member raised, or moved between `admin` and `build`, loses nothing. The lease is deleted and its revocation queued in the same turn as the change, and the alarm is set for now in that turn too, so the three are stored together. A role given again before the revocation is delivered is a new lease; the revocation still goes out.

**Delivery.** `SpaceDurableObject.alarm()` takes up to 16 of the queued revocations that are due, the longest due first, and for each calls `OverseerDurableObject.revokeSpaceAccess(profile)` on the workspace's Overseer or, for a lease given to a space, `SpaceDurableObject.revokeInherited(profile)` on that space. On arrival the workspace restarts if it remembers giving that profile a role, which it then forgets, and changes nothing otherwise, nor while it is being deleted (see "Delete"; docs/sharing.md, "Space roles"); a space takes back the leases it gave that profile on its own workspaces, which its own alarm then delivers. A revocation leaves the queue only once its call has returned. One whose call fails is logged (`space.revocation.deliver.failed`, with the workspace as `gadgetId` or the space as `spaceId`) and kept, due again after a wait of its own that doubles with each failure from one second to five minutes. That wait is stored with the revocation, so it does not start over when the space's object is discarded from memory, and it holds up no other: a revocation queued later is due at once and attempted in the next run, however many are waiting to be retried. While any remain the alarm is set for when the next is due, which is now while any is. No Overseer is ever polled: a workspace hears from its space only when a role the space gave out on it has ended.

## Sub-spaces

### The model

A space may hold sub-spaces, each a space in its own right: a `SpaceDurableObject` of its own, with its own id, name, members, listing of workspaces and sub-spaces. A space that is inside no other is a **root**. A personal space is a root that takes no members; a team space made with `createSpace` is a root with members. A sub-space is made with `Space.createSubspace(name)` in any space, a personal one included, and is always of kind "team": "personal" means only the one root each user has, so a sub-space of a personal space takes members while the personal space itself takes none. A sub-space may hold sub-spaces in turn, down to `MAX_SPACE_DEPTH` (5) levels below its root. It starts with no member of its own and need never have one, because the members of the spaces it is inside are its members.

The words this section uses: the **parent** of a sub-space is the space it was created in; its **ancestors** are the spaces it is inside, nearest first, from its parent to its root; and it is a **descendant** of each of them. A **member of the space itself** is someone `Space.setMemberRole` named there, or the creator of a root. A **member** is whoever holds a role in the space, given there or inherited.

### Roles are inherited

The role a user holds in a space is the higher of the role they were given there, if any, and the role they hold in its parent, `admin` over `build` over `use`. That comes to the highest role they were given in the space or in any of its ancestors, which is how it is computed (see "Ancestors, and why asking is one hop"). A role given in a sub-space never lowers an inherited one (`SpaceModel.roleOf(profileId, inherited)`):

| Given in the sub-space | Inherits nothing | Inherits `use` | Inherits `build` | Inherits `admin` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nothing | no role: refused | `use` | `build` | `admin` |
| `use` | `use` | `use` | `build` | `admin` |
| `build` | `build` | `build` | `build` | `admin` |
| `admin` | `admin` | `admin` | `admin` | `admin` |

So the owner of a personal space is an admin of every space below it, and an admin of a team space is an admin of every space below that one.

What a user may do in a space goes by the role they hold in it, in a sub-space as in a root:

| | Needs |
|---|---|
| Open the space; read its info, its own members, its workspaces and its sub-spaces; add workspaces of their own to it | any role (in a personal space, to be its owner) |
| Create a sub-space in it | `build` or `admin` |
| Give someone a role in it, change that role, or remove another member of the space itself | `admin`, and never in a personal space |
| Leave it | a role given in the space itself |

`setMemberRole` and `removeMember` change the roles given in the space they are called on, and no other. Someone made an admin of a sub-space alone manages the members that sub-space has of its own, and can do nothing about a member who inherits. The rule that a space keeps at least one admin guards a root only (`#keepAnAdmin` returns at once in a space whose record has `ancestors`). A root always has an admin, and every admin of a root is an admin of every space below it, so a sub-space is never without one, and the last admin it has of its own may be lowered, removed, or leave.

### Visibility

Someone added only to a sub-space reaches that sub-space and every space below it, and nothing else. Its parent and the sub-spaces beside it give them no role, so they refuse them with `noSuchSpace()`, as they refuse an id that names no space: the answer does not say that a space is there. `SpaceInfo.parent` is told only to a caller who inherits a role, who therefore holds a role in the parent and can open it (`SpaceModel.infoFor`). Someone added only to the sub-space gets its info with no `parent`, and no call returns the rest of `ancestors` to anyone.

`listSpaces()` and the mirror hold the spaces a user was given a role in, each with that role and no `parent` (see "The mirror"). So a sub-space someone was added to shows in their list as one more space, with nothing that links it to a space they cannot open, and a sub-space reached only by inheritance is not in the list. A member of a space finds its sub-spaces with `Space.listSubspaces()`, which returns the id and name of each sub-space created directly in it, and opens one with `openSpace(id)`.

### Ancestors, and why asking is one hop

A sub-space's record holds `ancestors`: the ids of the spaces it is inside, nearest first. The list is written once, when the sub-space is created, by the parent that creates it, as the parent's own id followed by the parent's own list (`SpaceModel.newSubspace`). Nothing moves a space and `create` changes nothing in a space that exists, so the list never changes. Its length is the sub-space's depth, at most `MAX_SPACE_DEPTH`, and its first entry is the parent, so neither is stored apart from it. A root has no such list.

A sub-space learns what a profile inherits by calling `SpaceDurableObject.roleFor(descendant, profileId)` on each of its ancestors directly, all at once, with its own id as `descendant`, and taking the highest answer (`SpaceDurableObject.#inherited`). An ancestor answers from its own member list alone (`SpaceModel.lend`) and calls no space: it does not ask the spaces above it, because the sub-space that asks is asking each of them itself. So the chain of Durable Object calls behind any decision in a sub-space is one call longer than in a root -- the space, then one ancestor -- whatever the depth. Five ancestors are five calls side by side, not five in a row.

That is why a sub-space stores the whole list and asks every space on it. The platform bounds how long one request's chain of calls may be, and a call refused at the bound fails with "Subrequest depth limit exceeded" (docs/sharing.md, "Terminating live sessions on revocation or scope growth", describes the error and the restart it triggers). The local runtime does not enforce the bound, so a chain that grew by a call with each level could not be shown to fit it by any test run here. This chain is the same length at every depth.

`SpaceModel` stays pure and makes no call. The shell asks and hands the model what was inherited, as the `inherited` argument of `roleOf(profileId, inherited)` and of the methods built on it. `inherited` is undefined in a root, when no ancestor gave the profile a role, and when the shell did not ask.

The shell does not ask when the profile's own role in the sub-space already settles the question. Each call names the role that is `enough`, and nobody is asked about a profile who was given at least that role in the sub-space itself:

| Call on the sub-space | Asks about | `enough` | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `open` | the caller | `use` | any role opens |
| `getInfo` | the caller | none: it always asks | the answer is the role shown, and also decides whether `parent` is told |
| `listMembers`, `listWorkspaces`, `listSubspaces` | the caller | `use` | any role reads |
| `setMemberRole` | the caller | `admin`, asked twice | only an admin changes members (see "Answers in flight") |
| `removeMember` | the caller | `use` for a caller removing themself, else `admin` | anyone leaves; only an admin removes another |
| `createSubspace` | the caller | `build` | `build` and `admin` create |
| `attachWorkspaces` | the workspaces' owner | `use` | any role adds workspaces |
| `workspaceRole` | the profile the workspace asks about | `build` | `admin` and `build` both give `build` on a workspace |

Nothing else asks an ancestor: `roleFor`, `revokeInherited`, `create`, `detachWorkspace`, the alarm and the mirror push decide nothing by an inherited role. So an admin of the sub-space itself is asked about by `getInfo` alone, a `build` member of it is not asked about where a workspace's role or the right to create a sub-space is in question, and any member of it is not asked about where only membership is.

A question that fails is not an answer. `#inherited` catches nothing: when the call to any ancestor is rejected, the call that asked fails with the same error, the answers of the ancestors that were reached do not stand in for the one that was not, and the sub-space writes no lease. It never reads as "no role", which would be a wrong answer about someone who may hold one. The ancestors that did answer keep the leases they wrote, and revoking one of those later only over-revokes.

### Leases and revocation between spaces

Each answer of `roleFor` that gives a role is a lease in the ancestor that gave it, `{ space, profile }` with the asker's id as `space`, kept beside the leases on its own workspaces and in the same collection (see "Leases and revocations"). An answer of no role leaves none, so of the ancestors a sub-space asks, only those that gave the profile a role remember the question.

When that profile is removed from the ancestor, or lowered there from `admin` or `build` to `use`, the ancestor takes back every lease of theirs, of both kinds, and queues each. Its alarm delivers a space's revocation by calling `revokeInherited(profileId)` on the space it answered, and repeats the call until it returns, as it repeats a workspace's. The space that is told takes back every lease it gave that profile on a workspace (`SpaceModel.revokeInherited`), and its own alarm delivers those to the workspaces' Overseers, which restart as they do when a role ends in a root. Each session reopens and is authorized afresh, so someone who still holds a role, given in the sub-space or inherited from another ancestor, opens again with it. A member moved between `admin` and `build` in an ancestor loses nothing below it, as in the ancestor itself: both give `build` on a workspace, and what an admin may do to a space's members is decided at each call.

Nothing passes from space to space beyond that one call. Every space that was answered is told by the ancestor that answered it, a space in between takes no part, and what the told space lent its own descendants stands, since it answered them from its own member list, which the revocation did not change.

Take a root R, a sub-space S1 in R, S2 in S1 and S3 in S2, a workspace that S3 lists, and a member given `build` in R who has that workspace open. For the open, S3 asked R, S2 and S1. Only R gave a role, so R holds the lease `{ space: S3, profile }`, S3 holds the lease on the workspace, and S1 and S2 hold nothing. Removing the member from R makes two deliveries: R's alarm calls `revokeInherited` on S3, and S3's alarm calls `revokeSpaceAccess` on the workspace's Overseer. S1 and S2 are not called. Had the member also opened S1 and S2, R would hold a lease for each and tell each itself, and neither would have a workspace lease to take back.

Over-revoking is safe: a session that is ended reopens and is authorized afresh. Under-revoking is not. So `revokeInherited` takes back every workspace lease of the profile, one that a role given in the sub-space still covers included, and a sub-space acts on it whichever space calls (see "Trust boundary").

### Answers in flight

A revocation can reach a sub-space while a question to its ancestors still awaits its answers, and those answers may have been given before the role was taken away. The sub-space does not trust them. `SpaceDurableObject` keeps the questions that are in flight, each with the profile it is about (`#asking`), and `revokeInherited(profileId)` marks those about its profile before it takes any lease back. A question that was marked discards its answers and asks every ancestor once more. If that second round is marked too, the call fails ("This space's inherited roles changed while one was being looked up."). A revocation for another profile marks nothing.

The call fails; it does not answer "no role". A workspace's Overseer treats a lookup that failed and an answer of no role differently: a failed lookup blocks an observation that excludes observers, and keeps the observer record of someone the workspace's own sharing dropped, where a wrong "no role" would let the observation through and remove the record (docs/sharing.md, "Space roles": "Observers" and "Lookups in flight"). So a sub-space that cannot vouch for an answer says so.

No answer is used after another `await`. In every method the model call that decides, which in `workspaceRole`, `attachWorkspaces`, `removeMember` and `setMemberRole` also writes, takes the awaited answer as its argument or is the next statement. So a revocation for the same profile lands either while the question is in flight, and is caught, or after the write, and takes the lease back like any later change. `setMemberRole` awaits a username lookup, and therefore asks twice: before the lookup (`requireMembershipAdmin`), so that only an admin learns whether an account exists, and again as an argument of the model call that writes the member, so that a caller whose role above ended during the lookup adds nobody. A caller who is an admin of the sub-space itself is asked about neither time, and a username with no account ends the call before the second. `createSubspace` is the one method that writes after an `await` which follows its asking (see "Creating a sub-space").

### Workspaces in a sub-space

`newWorkspace(spaceId)`, `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint(…, spaceId)` and `Overseer.moveToSpace(spaceId)` take a sub-space's id as they take any team space's. The id is recorded as the workspace's `spaceId`, and the owner's User DO registers the workspace with that sub-space, which lists it if the owner holds a role there, their own or inherited (`attachWorkspaces`). Everything in "Workspaces in a space" applies as it stands, the fallback and the workspaces a space never lists included: the User DO does not know whether the space it addresses is a root or a sub-space.

The role a user holds in a space applies to the workspaces that space lists, and to no workspace listed by its parent or by a space beside it. A member of a root opens a workspace that a sub-space lists because they hold a role in the sub-space, by inheritance: the workspace's Overseer asks the owner's User DO, which asks the sub-space the record points at, which asks its ancestors. Each space lists its own workspaces only, so `listWorkspaces()` on a root does not show those of the spaces below it.

### Creating a sub-space

`SpaceDurableObject.createSubspace(caller, name)` works in this order:

1. It trims and checks the name as `createSpace` does (`teamSpaceName()`).
2. It has `SpaceModel.newSubspace` build the sub-space's record, asking the ancestors about the caller first unless they were given `build` or `admin` in the space itself. `newSubspace` refuses a caller with no role (`noSuchSpace()`), then one who holds only `use` ("Only an admin or a build member of this space can create a sub-space."), and only then a space that already has `MAX_SPACE_DEPTH` ancestors ("A space can be nested at most 5 levels deep."), so someone with no role learns nothing of the depth. It writes nothing. The record is the name, kind "team" and `ancestors`: this space's id, then this space's own list.
3. It makes a fresh id (`newUniqueId()`) and calls `create(record)` on the space under it, with no creator.
4. It lists the sub-space in its own `children` (`SpaceModel.addSubspace`) and returns the entry, the sub-space's id and name (`SubspaceInfo`).

The sub-space exists before its parent lists it, so a creation that fails part way leaves a space that nothing lists and nobody reaches, never an entry that opens nothing. The listing is the one write made after an `await` without asking the ancestors again, and that is harmless: the entry of a sub-space with no member and no workspace gives nobody a role, and a caller whose role ended while the sub-space was being created leaves exactly what a creation that finished a moment earlier leaves.

`createSubspace` returns the sub-space's id and name, not the sub-space opened. A capability minted through the parent's object would have every later call on it pass through that object, and sub-spaces created one inside another through such capabilities would put one more object in the way at each level. A client opens the sub-space with `openSpace(id)`, which reaches it directly. The caller is not made a member of the sub-space itself: they keep the role they inherit, and their mirror gains no entry.

`SpaceModel.create` therefore has two shapes. A root takes a creator, who becomes its first admin (see "Personal and team spaces"). A sub-space, whose record holds `ancestors`, takes none and starts with no member. A call with neither a creator nor `ancestors` is refused ("A space inside no other needs a creator."), so no root is without an admin. `children` exists for members to find the sub-spaces: nothing is decided from an entry, since a sub-space's own record says which spaces it is inside.

### How this differs from a familiar shared-folder model

Sub-spaces behave like shared folders in what they inherit and in who sees what: a personal space is a root that is not shared, a team space is a shared root, and a sub-space takes the members of the space it is inside and may add its own, who see it and what is below it. Two things differ, deliberately. A workspace keeps its individual owner, and only the owner moves or deletes it, whatever role anyone holds in the space (see "Access through membership"). And a role in a space gives no power to share a workspace, which the workspace's own sharing alone decides (docs/sharing.md, "Space roles").

### Not in this version

- **Deleting, renaming or moving a sub-space.** `ancestors` is written once and `children` only gains entries.
- **Listing the spaces a user reaches only by inheritance.** `listSpaces()` and the mirror hold the spaces they were given a role in; the rest are found from a space above, with `listSubspaces()`.
- **Listing inherited members.** `listMembers()` returns the members a space has of its own, so a sub-space may list nobody.

### Tests

`packages/workshop-backend/__tests__/spaces-nested.test.ts` covers the model and the Durable Objects: the role held for every pair of own and inherited role, who may create a sub-space, change members and add workspaces, the depth limit and the `ancestors` each sub-space is given, the lease per answer, revocation across levels, answers in flight, a question that fails, and what someone added only to a sub-space is refused. It also asserts the shape of the calls: for a decision in the deepest sub-space, `roleFor` is called once on each ancestor, each time by that sub-space, and `revokeInherited` on no space, so a change that had an ancestor ask on would fail there. `packages/integration-tests/__tests__/workshop-subspaces.test.ts` tells the same story over the real API: sub-spaces listed and opened by inherited roles, what someone added only to a sub-space reaches, the higher of two roles and the one left when the other ends, a workspace opened and used in a sub-space as deep as the limit allows by members of its root, who lose it with their role while holding a session there, a sub-space of a personal space, and the workspaces a sub-space never lists.

## Where the state lives

**One Durable Object per space.** Each space is a `SpaceDurableObject`, addressed by the space's id. Its storage (`storage-schema/space-storage.ts`) is a singleton `info` -- the name, kind, for a personal space the owner and for a sub-space its `ancestors` (see "Sub-spaces"), but no id, which is the object's own; absent until the space is created and never removed -- a collection `members` keyed by profile id, which holds the members the space has of its own, a collection `workspaces` keyed by workspace id (the listing, see "Workspaces in a space"), a collection `children` keyed by space id (the id and name of each sub-space created in it), and two collections behind the roles its members hold on those workspaces and in the spaces below it, `leases` and `revocations`, with the counter `nextRevocation` (see "Leases and revocations"). A personal space's owner is stored in `members` too, as its only admin and only member.

**Nothing lists the spaces.** No object lists the spaces of a deployment. A space is reached by its id, which a member reads from their own list of spaces or, for a sub-space, from the `children` of a space they hold a role in (`Space.listSubspaces()`), and whether an id names a space is asked of the object under it.

**The user's own Durable Object** holds (`storage-schema/user-storage.ts`) its records of the user's workspaces, each with its `spaceId` and its marker (see "Workspaces in a space"), and `spaces`, a **mirror** of the roles the user was given: one `SpaceInfo` per space they were given a role in, keyed by space id, with `role` set to that role and no `parent`. A space the user reaches only by inheritance is never in it. The mirror authorizes nothing. Nothing is stored to find the personal space, whose id is derived from the user's profile id. Once the mirror holds the personal space, `listSpaces()` is answered from the user's own object alone, waiting on no call to any space.

### The mirror

The mirror is presentation only, like the record a recipient's account keeps of a workspace shared with them (`UserDurableObject.recordSharedWorkspaceOpen()`, see docs/sharing.md). Every operation on a space is decided from what the space itself stores at the time of the call -- its `info`, its member list and, for an entry of its listing, the owner that entry is listed under (see "Workspaces in a space") -- and, in a sub-space, from what its ancestors answer within that call; nothing is ever authorized from the mirror.

The space keeps it current by pushing to the affected user's object -- `recordSpaceMembership(info)` after `setMemberRole`, `forgetSpace(id)` after a `removeMember` that removed someone. What it pushes is the space as the user's own member record gives it (`SpaceModel.infoFor(profileId)`, with no inherited role): the role they were given there, which the role they hold may exceed, and never `parent`. The push is best-effort: the member list is already written, so a push that fails is logged (`space.membership.mirror.failed`) and the change stands. Opening is what heals a lost push: before `SpaceDurableObject.open` answers, the space makes the same push for the caller, recording the role they were given there if they have one and dropping the space from their mirror if they do not, which for someone who opens a sub-space by an inherited role alone drops nothing. So a listed space may still refuse to open, and a membership the mirror missed appears once that space has been opened by id. A space that was never created pushes nothing: it has never had a member, so no mirror holds it.

### Trust boundary

Every method `SpaceDurableObject` exposes takes the acting user as a plain parameter -- their profile id, or for `create` and `attachWorkspaces` their profile -- like `OverseerDurableObject.open()`. Its only callers are `AuthenticatedApiImpl` (server.ts), which passes the identity of the authenticated session, `UserDurableObject` (user.ts), which passes its own, and another `SpaceDurableObject`, which states its own id -- never a client, gadget, gatekeeper or agent -- so the parameters are authoritative.

`attachWorkspaces`, `detachWorkspace` and `workspaceRole` are called by `UserDurableObject` alone, which states its own user as the owner, so a user's workspaces are only ever registered or dropped by that user's object, and a space is only ever asked for a role on a workspace by the object whose record places it there. None is on `Space`. `UserDurableObject.workspaceRoleInSpace` in turn is called only by a workspace's Overseer, about itself, and `OverseerDurableObject.revokeSpaceAccess` only by a space's alarm; all it can do is end sessions, which then reopen and are authorized afresh.

One `SpaceDurableObject` calls another in three places, with methods that are not on `Space` either. A parent calls `create` on the sub-space it makes, stating that sub-space's ancestors. A sub-space calls `roleFor` on each of its ancestors, stating its own id. An ancestor's alarm calls `revokeInherited` on a space it answered, and all that can do is end sessions, which then reopen and are authorized afresh.

- **No client chooses whom a space asks.** A record with `ancestors` is written only by `SpaceModel.create`, which only `SpaceDurableObject.create` calls, and that has three call sites. `AuthenticatedApiImpl.createSpace` and `UserDurableObject.#ensurePersonalSpace()` pass a name and a kind and no ancestors. `SpaceDurableObject.createSubspace` passes the record `SpaceModel.newSubspace` built, whose `ancestors` are the creating space's own id (`ctx.id`) followed by its own stored list, to a space under an id it made in the same call. The only value a client supplies to any of the three is the name. `create` changes nothing in a space that exists, so a list once written is never rewritten.
- **An ancestor answers whichever space asks.** A Durable Object call carries no identity of its caller, so the id an asker states is all an ancestor has, and an ancestor records only the sub-spaces created directly in it (`children`), not the ones further down: it cannot tell which spaces are below it. It does not try. Only kernel code can ask and the answer returns to kernel code, which uses it for a profile in a space whose stored `ancestors` name the ancestor asked. The stated id only says where a revocation goes, and a `revokeInherited` that reaches a space which never asked takes back that profile's workspace leases there, which over-revokes and is safe.

A client only ever holds a `Space`: the `SpaceClientInterface` minted by `SpaceDurableObject.open()`, which closes over the caller fixed at open time. It carries no standing permission. Every method has the space look that user's membership up again, so a stub held by someone who has since been removed or demoted loses those powers at once, with no session to terminate. A workspace they have open is another matter: that session was authorized when it opened, and is what a revocation ends.

`setMemberRole` checks that the caller is an admin *before* it resolves the username, so a non-admin cannot use it to find out which accounts exist. The lookup is the one `Overseer.addCollaborator` uses (`UserDurableObject.whoamiIfExists()`), and likewise returns null for a username with no account. In a sub-space that check may ask the ancestors, and they are asked again after the lookup, before the member is written (see "Sub-spaces", "Answers in flight").

## API surface

- `AuthenticatedApi.listSpaces()` -- the spaces the caller was given a role in, from their mirror: their personal space first, then the others by name, each with that role and no `parent`. A space they reach only by inheritance is not listed. It creates the caller's personal space if it does not exist yet.
- `AuthenticatedApi.openSpace(id)` -- a `Space` acting as the caller. A string that is no space id throws; an id the caller cannot open gets the single refusal (see "Ids").
- `AuthenticatedApi.createSpace(name)` -- creates a team space under an id the server makes and opens it, with the caller as its first admin.
- `Space` -- for any member: `getInfo()`, whose `role` is the higher of the role given in the space and the one inherited; `listMembers()`, which lists the members the space has of its own; `listWorkspaces()`; and `listSubspaces()`, the sub-spaces created directly in the space, by name. For `build` members and admins: `createSubspace(name)`, which returns the sub-space's `SubspaceInfo`, its id and name, and not the sub-space opened. `setMemberRole(username, role)` for admins of a team space; `removeMember(profileId)` for an admin, or a member of the space itself removing themself. The last two also change who may open the workspaces listed by the space and by the spaces below it.
- `SpaceInfo.parent` -- the id of the space a sub-space was created in, told by `getInfo()` only to a caller who holds a role in that space, and never in an entry of `listSpaces()`.
- `AuthenticatedApi.openWorkspace(id)` -- opens, besides a workspace the caller owns or one shared with them, one that belongs to a space they are a member of and that the space lists (see "Access through membership"). `WorkspaceMetadata.role` on the session is what the membership and the workspace's own sharing give, joined, and does not say which it came from.
- `AuthenticatedApi.newWorkspace(spaceId?)` and `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint(blueprintId, bindings, spaceId?)` -- create the workspace in a team space, a sub-space included; omitted, or given the id of the caller's own personal space, in the caller's personal space. A string that is no space id throws, and no workspace is created.
- `Overseer.moveToSpace(spaceId | null)` -- owner only: moves the workspace to a team space, or with `null`, or the id of the owner's own personal space, back to its owner's personal space.
- `WorkspaceMetadata.spaceId` -- the id of the team space a workspace belongs to, on its owner's own record only (so in `listWorkspaces()` for a workspace the caller owns); absent means the owner's personal space.

## Known limitations

- **A space cannot be deleted, renamed or moved.** Nothing deletes a space, a sub-space included, nothing changes a team space's name, nothing moves a sub-space to another parent, and a personal space's name is the snapshot taken when it was created.
- **Inheritance is not listed.** A user's list of spaces (`listSpaces()`) does not show the spaces they reach through a space above, which they find from that space with `listSubspaces()`. A space's member list (`listMembers()`) does not show who holds a role in it by inheritance.
- **The mirror shows the role given, not the role held.** An entry of `listSpaces()` for a sub-space carries the role the user was given there, which the role they hold may exceed; `getInfo()` reports the one they hold.
- **A sub-space with no admin of its own tells its members that a space is above it.** `listMembers()` there may show no admin, or nobody, which only inherited admins explain. Someone added only to a sub-space still cannot learn which space that is. A `build` member can also learn how many levels are above: `createSubspace` is refused at the depth limit with a text that names the limit, so counting how deep they can nest tells them.
- **A sub-space needs all of its ancestors to answer.** Every question a sub-space has to ask is put to each ancestor, and one that cannot be reached fails the call for everyone whose own role there does not settle it, even when a nearer ancestor would have given them a role. That includes the update of a workspace the sub-space already lists (a title change, the sync after activity) when its owner holds no role of their own in the sub-space: it fails and is retried by the next sync (`space.workspace.sync.failed`), although a listed workspace needs no role to stay listed.
- **A role lookup in a sub-space is one Durable Object call longer than in a root.** That holds at any depth (see "Ancestors, and why asking is one hop"). If the platform refuses a call on that way, the refusal is passed on unchanged, so the workspace's Overseer treats it as any such refusal of a call to its owner's User DO (docs/sharing.md, the paragraph on `restartIfLoopLimited`). The local runtime does not enforce the platform's bound, so the suites exercise which calls are made, at the deepest level included, and not whether the platform accepts them.
- **A revocation from an ancestor waits for the sub-space it answered.** Until that sub-space's `revokeInherited` returns, the delivery is retried after waits that double up to five minutes (`REVOCATION_RETRY_MS`), and the sessions below stay open, although every fresh open is already decided without the role. It holds up no other delivery.
- **A revocation from an ancestor restarts workspaces that a role given in the sub-space still covers.** The sub-space takes back every workspace lease of that profile, whatever it rested on; the member reopens with the role they still hold.
- **A member can be turned away once while a role above them is being revoked.** A question to the ancestors that revocations for the same profile overtake twice in a row fails the call (see "Answers in flight"). At an open of a workspace the Overseer counts that as a lookup that failed, so the member opens with what the workspace's own sharing gives them, or is refused if it gives nothing, and opens by their role on trying again.
- **A sub-space whose creation failed part way can exist with no space listing it.** It has no member and no workspace, its id was returned to nobody, and nothing reaches it; nothing removes it either.
- **Unread state and notifications skip members.** The unread counts and notification fan-out of a workspace go to its owner and to the `build` collaborators of its own sharing (`deliverDirtyChats`, `mayReadChatState` in `overseer.ts`). Someone who reaches it only through a space gets neither, and it is not in their own list of workspaces.
- **Names are not unique.** Two team spaces may carry the same name, and only the id tells them apart. A `createSpace` that is repeated after its answer was lost makes a second space.
- **Display names are snapshots.** The display name in a member record, in `SpaceInfo.owner`, in a personal space's `name` and in a listed workspace's `owner` may trail a rename. A listed workspace's title trails a change until its next sync.
- **Ending a role restarts the whole workspace.** A revocation disconnects every session of the workspace, the owner's included, not only the affected member's. It does so whenever the workspace's Overseer, since it last started, gave that member a role, whether or not anything of theirs is still open: it may have learned the role while checking an observation that excludes them, and it cannot tell that a client kept nothing a closed session handed out. Each client reopens and is authorized afresh.
- **A session outlives its role for a moment.** Between the change in the space and the alarm delivering the revocation, a session already open continues, and longer while the workspace's Overseer cannot be reached: the revocation is retried, with waits of up to five minutes, for as long as it takes. A workspace on its way to another space keeps the sessions opened through the space it is leaving until that space drops its entry, which a failed detach puts off to the next sync. Opens are not subject to this window, since each asks the space as it stands.
- **Leases are only ever revoked.** A lease stays until its member is removed or lowered, its workspace leaves the listing, or an ancestor takes back a role the lease may have rested on, whether or not the session it was given for is still open, so a workspace leaving a listing queues one revocation for every lease still standing on it: one for each member answered with a role on it since their leases were last revoked. (A member lowered to `use` had theirs revoked then, and holds one again only if answered with a role afterwards.) Its Overseer ignores each one for a member it gave no role since it last started and is not asking about, and every one while it is being deleted (see "Delete").
- **A late revocation restarts a workspace whose member still has a role.** A revocation that arrives after its member regained a role by another route, for example on a workspace moved between two spaces they belong to and reopened before the space it left was heard from, finds that role remembered and restarts the workspace once; the member reopens with the access they had.
- **A member can be turned away once while roles are being revoked.** At an open, a lookup that revocations of other members' roles overtake twice in a row counts as no space role (docs/sharing.md, "Lookups in flight"), so in that rare interleaving a member opens with what the workspace's own sharing gives them, or is refused if it gives nothing, and opens as a member on trying again.
- **A workspace does not show who reaches it through its space.** Its collaborator list holds only the people its own sharing admits, and a session does not say whether its role came from the space, so a member who builds through the space and tries to share finds out from the refusal. The Share modal of an owner-invites-only workspace says that only the owner can add people, which is true of its collaborators: the admins of its space still admit members, who open it in their role.
- **A listing shows a workspace to members before they can open it.** Every member of a space sees the title, owner and creation date of each workspace it lists, including a member who has not passed, or cannot pass, the verification that opening it takes (docs/observers.md), a workspace that holds restricted data included. The title shown may be one a model generated from the workspace's chat.
- **A workspace cannot be evicted.** It stays in a team space after its owner leaves or is removed, and only its owner can move or delete it: a space admin cannot take it off the listing. The space's members keep their roles on it meanwhile, and its owner, no longer a member, still owns it.
- **Membership is checked at registration, not at creation.** `newWorkspace(spaceId)` and `newWorkspaceFromBlueprint(…, spaceId)` accept any space id, and a workspace whose owner may not add to that space falls back to their personal space at its first activity (for one made from a blueprint, as it is created), with nothing reported to the caller.
- **A workspace can be listed by two spaces.** During a move both list it between the attach and the detach, and when either call fails, possibly until the next sync. Until then both show it, though only the members of the space its record points at can open it through their membership.
- **A deleted workspace can stay listed for a moment.** While a space cannot be reached, its entry and the owner's record stand until the workspace's repeated removal gets through (see "Delete"); opening the workspace is refused as not found meanwhile.
- **A collaborator's Outputs index can outlast a space role.** For someone who is both a collaborator and a member, the index is written from everything they can open at the time, at each open and while their session lasts. Gadgets reached only through the space stay in it until their next open after the role ends, and opening one is refused.
- **The listing is not paginated.** `listWorkspaces()` returns every workspace of the space in one answer.
- **A lost mirror write hides a space until it is opened.** A member whose mirror write was lost finds the space again by opening it by id. A team space whose creator received no answer to `createSpace`, and whose mirror write was lost as well, cannot be found: nothing lists the spaces, and nobody holds its id.
